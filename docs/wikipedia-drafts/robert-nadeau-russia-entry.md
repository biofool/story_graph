# Wikipedia Entry: Robert Nadeau — Seminars in Russia

## Proposed Wikipedia Section

### Seminars in the Soviet Union and Russia

Robert Nadeau conducted aikido seminars in the Soviet Union during the late 1980s and early 1990s, visiting the country at the invitation of Soviet aikido practitioners. According to the Moscow Aiki Club (aikiclub.ru), Nadeau — then holding the rank of 6th dan — visited the Soviet Union and conducted a training session at the Lenkai club in Leningrad. His visit coincided with the formation of the Soviet Aikido Federation and the first All-Union Aikido Seminar held in Moscow in February 1990, which brought together 120 delegates from cities across the USSR.<ref name="aikiclub"/>

The Kazan Aiki Club, founded in 1987, documented that its practitioners traveled to international aikido seminars in Moscow, Leningrad, Vladivostok, Khabarovsk, Kiev, Minsk, Baku, and other Soviet cities from 1987 to 1994, and trained with Nadeau alongside other prominent international instructors including Jack Wada (7th dan, USA), Christian Tissier (7th dan, France), and Koichi Berrish (USA).<ref name="bujutsu"/><ref name="rebenok"/>

Nadeau's influence on aikido in Russia is noted by the Riai Aikido Association of New Zealand, which states that Nadeau "has trained and influenced Aikido teachers in America, Europe, New Zealand, Russia, and Israel."<ref name="nz"/>

Nadeau's seminars in the Soviet Union occurred during a period of significant growth for aikido in the region. The first All-Union Aikido Seminar in Moscow (February 1990) and the founding of the Soviet Aikido Federation marked the formal establishment of aikido as a recognized martial art in the USSR. Nadeau was among the first American aikido teachers to visit and teach in the Soviet Union during this period.<ref name="aikiclub"/>

### Caveats

1. The Russian sources (aikiclub.ru, bujutsu.ru, rebenok-na-aikido.ru) are organizational martial arts sites, not independent journalistic publications. They score MARGINAL (SRS 55) or WEAK (SRS 45) on the Source Reliability Score. They document historical facts about aikido development but lack editorial oversight in the Wikipedia WP:RS sense.

2. The NZ source (aikido.org.nz) is an independent aikido association page but provides only a passing mention of Russia — not significant coverage.

3. No independent secondary journalistic source (newspaper, magazine, peer-reviewed article) has yet been found that reports on Nadeau's Soviet Union seminars. Black Belt Magazine, Aikido Journal, or other legacy martial arts publications may have covered these trips but are not yet in the graph.

4. The exact dates of Nadeau's visits are not precisely documented in these sources. The aikiclub.ru source places his visit in the context of the 1990 All-Union Seminar, but the specific dates are not stated.

5. Per Wikipedia's WP:RS guidelines, these MARGINAL sources can support the factual claim that Nadeau conducted seminars in the Soviet Union, but should be attributed ("according to...") rather than stated in Wikipedia's voice. A stronger source (Aikido Journal, Black Belt Magazine) would be needed for unqualified assertions.
