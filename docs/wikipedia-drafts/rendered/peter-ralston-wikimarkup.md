'''Peter Ralston''' is an American martial arts teacher and author founder of Cheng Hsin, an internal martial arts and bodywork system. In 1978, he won the light-heavyweight division at the Second World Kuoshu Martial Arts Tournament in Taiwan, according to contemporary newspaper and magazine accounts.<ref name="tribune1978"/><ref name="cressy"/>

The ''[[Oakland Tribune]]'' reported that Ralston was the first American to win a championship at the tournament.<ref name="tribune1978"/> A contemporary feature in ''[[Black Belt (magazine)|Black Belt]]'' and later articles in ''[[Parade (magazine)|Parade]]'' and ''Blitz'' described him as the first non-Asian competitor to win the event.<ref name="cressy"/><ref name="significa"/><ref name="blitz"/>

== Early life ==

Ralston was born in [[San Francisco]] and spent part of his childhood in Asia, where his father worked.<ref name="significa"/> In an interview published in the ''Parade'' column ''Significa'', he said that a friend persuaded him to take judo classes in Singapore when he was nine. He also described a later insight into [[tai chi|t'ai chi ch'uan]] and the idea that the art should not be based on struggle.<ref name="significa"/>

== Martial arts career ==

Ralston founded Cheng Hsin in 1975. A USAdojo profile states that he opened the Cheng Hsin School of Internal Martial Arts and Center for Ontological Research in Oakland, California, in 1977.<ref name="usadojo"/> The founding date and institutional history should preferably be supported by an independent contemporary source.

== Teaching and writing ==

Ralston's Cheng Hsin work combines martial arts instruction with bodywork, facilitation, and studies of consciousness.
