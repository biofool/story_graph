== Career ==

Robert Nadeau founded Aikido Association of Northern California, California Aikido Association, Menlo Park Dojo, San Francisco dojo.<ref name="ref25"/>

More important, it was noted by many that his attitude changed each time he returned from Hawaii.<ref name="ref15"/>

Again, if I hadn’t met O-Sensei I would have followed this man because of his energy capability plus his fighting skills. But again, O-Sensei was so much more than all of these guys.<ref name="ref15"/>

I knew he was out of town a lot.<ref name="ref15"/>

It was later that the questions came up of Tohei Sensei’s changing things.<ref name="ref15"/>

AANC principal instructors: Nadeau, Doran, Witt<ref name="ref19"/>

AJ Encyclopedia: Nadeau b.1940, 7th dan, Hombu early 1960s, AANC leader<ref name="ref5"/>

Nadeau's Human Potential Movement circle (Murphy, Leonard, Frager, Esalen)<ref name="ref22"/>

After a while it was just the aikido that interested me. Slowly, I dropped out of the other arts. It could have turned out differently.<ref name="ref15"/>

Actually, my point was that he was not in charge of the technical curriculum. That’s true.<ref name="ref15"/>

I think that period was the real beginning of aikido in Northern California.<ref name="ref15"/>

Morihei Ueshiba, who would remain a constant source of inspiration and guidance to the young foreigner.<ref name="ref15"/>

Koichi Tohei was a very good teacher.<ref name="ref15"/>

I was very impressed with O-Sensei…<ref name="ref15"/>

At no time did I ever feel anything on his part but a desire to communicate and be honest with me. Never did I get any negative reaction to my questions.<ref name="ref15"/>

Not everyone reacted positively.<ref name="ref15"/>

We used to talk about it in the nearby coffee shop, the Kojimaya. We would talk about what was going to happen when O-Sensei died.<ref name="ref15"/>

The previously mentioned psychic said, 'You will live with him, above him, or near him.'<ref name="ref15"/>

But now that you bring up the subject of ranking, it’s so screwy today!<ref name="ref15"/>

I don’t know if he was the dominant force, but he certainly was a dominant force; remember he was the head teacher.<ref name="ref15"/>

George Leonard was first taught aikido by Nadeau<ref name="ref17"/>
