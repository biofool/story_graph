## Peter Ralston

{{Draft article|Peter Ralston (martial artist)}}
{{WikiProject Biography|living=yes|martial-arts=yes|aikido=yes}}

'''Peter Ralston''' (born October 29, 1949) is an American martial artist, consciousness teacher,
and author. He is the founder of the [[Cheng Hsin]] School of Internal Martial Arts and Center for
Ontological Research, based in [[Oakland, California]]. In 1978, Ralston became the first non-Asian
to win the World Championship full-contact [[martial arts]] tournament held in the [[Republic of
China]] ([[Taiwan]]).<ref name="blackbelt-1978">{{cite journal

He is the author of several books on consciousness and martial arts, including ''The Book of Not
Knowing'' (2010), ''Zen Body-Being'' (2006), and ''Cheng Hsin: The Principles of Effortless Power''

== Early life and martial arts training ==

Ralston was born on October 29, 1949 at St. Mary's Hospital in [[San Francisco]], [[California]],
but was raised primarily in [[Asia]].<ref name="wikitia-bio">{{cite web
  | url = https://www.wikitia.com/wiki/Peter_Ralston
  | title = Peter Ralston
  | publisher = Wikitia
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref> He began studying martial arts at the age of nine in [[Singapore]], starting with
[[Judo]]. By the age of twenty, he had earned [[black belt]]s in [[Judo]] ({{lang|ja|nidan}}),
[[Jujitsu]] ({{lang|ja|nidan}}), and [[Karate]] ({{lang|ja|shodan}}). He was the [[Sumo]] champion at
his high school in [[Japan]] and the Judo and [[fencing]] champion at the [[University of
California, Berkeley|UC Berkeley]], where he majored in Physiology/Anatomy.<ref name="chenghsin-bio"
/><ref name="nab-bio" /><ref name="books-org">{{cite web
  | url = https://books.org/books/cheng-hsin-tui-shou-the-art-of-effortless-power/peter-ralston/9781556430947/
  | title = Cheng Hsin Tui Shou: The Art of Effortless Power — Author biography
  | publisher = Books.org
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

His early training also included [[Kempo]], [[Ch'uan Fa]], and [[Northern Sil Lum Kung Fu]]. A
growing interest in [[internal martial arts]] led him to study [[T'ai Chi Ch'uan|T'ai Chi Ch'üan]],
[[Hsing I Ch'uan|Xingyiquan]], and [[Pa Kua Chang|Baguazhang]], as well as [[Aikido]], Japanese and
Chinese fencing, [[western boxing]], and [[Muay Thai]].<ref name="chenghsin-bio" /><ref name="nab-bio" />

== Training with Wong Jack-man ==

In 1969, at the age of 19, Ralston began studying under [[Wong Jack-man]] ({{zh|c=黃澤民}}), a
grandmaster of [[Northern Shaolin]], [[Tai chi|Taijiquan]], and [[Xingyiquan|Xingyiquan]] who is best
known for his controversial 1964 duel with [[Bruce Lee]] in [[Oakland, California|Oakland]].<ref
name="wong-wiki">{{cite web
  | url = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wong_Jack-man
  | title = Wong Jack-man
  | publisher = Wikipedia
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref> Ralston trained at Wong's school, the Chinese Physical Culture Association ({{zh|c=精武體育會|p=Jīngwǔ Tǐyùhuì}}), located at 880 Pacific Avenue in [[Chinatown, San Francisco|San Francisco's Chinatown]], for approximately twelve years (ages 19 to 31).<ref name="ralston-chinatown">{{cite web
  | url = https://chenghsin.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Chinatown-Fights-1970s.pdf
  | title = True Stories of Fights in 1970's Chinatown, San Francisco
  | author = Ralston, Peter
  | publisher = Cheng Hsin
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
  | format = PDF
}}</ref><ref name="taichi-1987">{{cite journal
  | title = Peter Ralston on his inner work
  | journal = T'ai Chi Magazine
  | volume = 11
  | issue = 3
  | date = June 1987
}}</ref>

With Wong, Ralston studied Northern Chinese martial arts including Northern Sil Lum Kung Fu,
[[Praying Mantis (martial art)|Praying Mantis]], [[Lohan (martial art)|Lohan]], T'ai Chi, Hsing I,
and Pa Kua, along with numerous weapons. In later years he also experimented with [[Wing Chun]],
Kempo, [[Hung Ga|Hung Gar]], and [[Muay Thai]].<ref name="en-academic">{{cite web
  | url = https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/10270380
  | title = Peter Ralston
  | publisher = Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

Ralston was, for many years, the only non-Chinese student at the school. This has been independently
corroborated by Stieg Hedlund, a game designer and martial artist who was taught by Ralston and
described him as "an early student at The Chinese Physical Culture Association" and a Caucasian
among predominantly Chinese students.<ref name="hedlund">{{cite web
  | url = https://medium.com/deru-kugi/the-bruce-lie-168e7c7066a0
  | title = The Bruce Lie — Showdown in Oaktown (Mythmaking in the Martial Arts, Part 1)
  | author = Hedlund, Stieg
  | publisher = Medium
  | date = 2017-09-18
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref> The Ralston–Wong lineage is also documented through David Tircuit, a martial arts teacher
who apprenticed with Ralston at the Cheng Hsin School from ages 11 to 18 and subsequently studied
with Wong Jack-man from ages 18 to 30.<ref name="tircuit">{{cite web
  | url = https://www.terracinaherbals.com/about
  | title = David Tircuit, L.Ac. — Biography
  | publisher = Terracina Aromatics & Herbals
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

Ralston has written that Wong contributed significantly to his skills in private sessions, but that
he went beyond Wong's teachings to develop the skills that made him world class.<ref
name="ralston-chinatown" />

== Cheng Hsin School ==

In 1975, Ralston founded the '''Cheng Hsin School''' ({{zh|c=誠心|p=Chéngxīn|l=sincere heart}}), a
martial arts and consciousness research school. In 1977, he opened a center called The Cheng Hsin
School of Internal Martial Arts and Center for Ontological Research on Telegraph Avenue in
[[Oakland, California]]. The school teaches the "Art of Effortless Power," an internal martial art
that uses principles of joining, complementing, and leading to develop interactive skills.<ref
name="chenghsin-bio" /><ref name="en-academic" />

In developing Cheng Hsin, Ralston also joined a professional boxing gym in Oakland and took up
[[Aikido]] with [[Robert Nadeau (aikidoka)|Robert Nadeau]] to expand his exposure to different
arts.<ref name="en-academic" /> Nadeau, a direct student of [[Morihei Ueshiba|Aikido founder Morihei
Ueshiba]], is the subject of the book ''Aikido: The Art of Transformation'', for which Ralston
contributed an endorsement: "There is much to learn from Robert. His emphasis on experiential
development by deeply understanding and applying powerful principles to life is transforming for
everybody."<ref name="nadeau-site">{{cite web
  | url = https://www.nadeaushihan.com/
  | title = Robert Nadeau Shihan — Aikido: The Art of Transformation
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref> [[Richard Moon (aikidoka)|Richard Moon]], a lifelong Aikido student of Nadeau's, also
studied tai chi and boxing with Ralston.<ref name="moon-qa">{{cite web
  | url = https://quantumaikido.com/
  | title = Quantum Aikido: The Power of Harmony — Richard Moon
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

Cheng Hsin has affiliated schools in [[Australia]], [[Germany]], [[France]], [[Hungary]], the
[[Netherlands]], [[South Korea]], [[Sweden]], and [[Wales]].<ref name="chenghsin-site">{{cite web
  | url = https://chenghsin.com
  | title = Cheng Hsin — Official Site
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

== 1978 World Championship ==

In 1978, Ralston became the first non-Asian ever to win the World Championship full-contact martial
arts tournament held in the Republic of China (Taiwan). The tournament was held in [[Taipei]] over
five days, with full-power striking and throws permitted without protective gear.<ref
name="en-academic" /><ref name="holland-interview">{{cite web
  | url = https://chenghsin.com/peter-ralston-holland-interview/
  | title = Peter Ralston Holland Interview
  | publisher = Cheng Hsin
  | date = 2017-01-25
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref> This achievement is noted in three independent martial-arts magazines:

* Scott Cressy, "Cheng Hsin: Essence of All Martial Arts," ''Black Belt Magazine'', December 1978,
  pp. 46–49 — "For the first time in the history and tradition of any Chinese World Tournament, a
  non-Asian won the championship."<ref name="blackbelt-1978" />
* Michael Dorgan, "Kung Fu: Approaching the moment to self improvement," ''Karate Illustrated'',
  June 1981, pp. 34–39 — "In 1978 Peter Ralston became the first non-Asian to win a gold medal at the
  World Championship full-contact martial arts tournament in the Republic of China."<ref
  name="karate-illust-1981" />
* Mark Grzic, "The Man Behind Bloodsport," ''Blitz Magazine'', August 2011, vol. 25, pp. 70–74 —
  "Fighting in a competition that had previously been marred by the deaths of competitors, in 1978
  Peter Ralston became the first non-Asian to win the full-contact Martial Arts World Tournament in
  China."<ref name="blitz-2011">{{cite journal
  | last = Grzic
  | first = Mark

Ralston's victory is also documented on his official biography and in publisher profiles.<ref

== Consciousness work ==

Ralston is described as one of the founders of the [[consciousness movement]] that began in the
[[San Francisco Bay Area]] in the late 1960s and early 1970s. He worked with [[Stewart Emery]] in
[[Actualizations]] and helped [[Werner Erhard]] develop a shift in his work from [[EST]] to
developing [[The Forum]]. He spent thousands of hours in [[Zen]] contemplation and participated in
dozens of intensives with [[Charles Berner]], the founder of the [[Enlightenment
Intensive]].<ref name="chenghsin-bio" /><ref name="noyes-book">{{cite book
  | last = Noyes
  | first = Lawrence
  | title = The Enlightenment Intensive: Dyad Communication as a Tool for Self-Realization
  | publisher = Frog Ltd.
  | year = 1998
  | page = 129
  | isbn = 1-883319-73-0
  | oclc = 39024629
}}</ref><ref name="origins">{{cite web
  | url = https://chenghsin.com/origins-interview-with-peter-ralston/
  | title = Origins Interview with Peter Ralston
  | publisher = Cheng Hsin
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

After several enlightenment experiences in the early 1970s, Ralston's teaching approach has been to
lead students away from beliefs and toward direct personal experience and discovery. He has
facilitated workshops for [[Esalen]], Lifesprings, and [[Tony Robbins]]' NLP trainings.

== Early life and training ==

Noha was born in [[Chicago]] to [[Czechoslovakia]]n-American parents. His father was a
parachutist with the U.S. Airborne forces during [[World War II]]. The family moved frequently and
eventually settled in [[Maryland]] near his father's military base.<ref name="argus">{{cite news
  | url = https://www.aikidopetaluma.com/2015/09/20/profile-robert-noha-article-from-the-argus-courier-may-14-2003/
  | title = Profile: Robert Noha
  | newspaper = Argus Courier
  | date = 2003-05-14
  | access-date = 2026-09-08
}}</ref>

Noha began practicing Aikido in 1966 at the Mountain View Aikikai in [[Mountain View, California]],
under [[Ed Riggs]] and [[Sig Kufferath]]. Shortly thereafter, he began training with [[Robert Nadeau
(aikidoka)|Robert Nadeau]], which started a lifelong friendship and teacher-student relationship.
His first martial arts training was in [[boxing]] and [[Kenpo]] [[Karate]] under Harvey Cleary, a
student of [[Ed Parker]].<ref name="maytt">{{cite interview
  | last = Noha
  | first = Robert
  | title = Interview with Californian Aikidoka Robert Noha: Aikido's Spiritual Aspect
  | work = Martial Arts of Yesterday, Today and Tomorrow
  | date = 2021-02-17
  | url = https://maytt.home.blog/2021/02/17/interview-with-californian-aikidoka-robert-noha-aikidos-spiritual-aspect/
  | access-date = 2026-09-08
}}</ref>

Noha enrolled in the [[University of Maryland]], majoring in [[philosophy]], and served in the
[[United States Air Force|U.S. Air Force]] reserves.<ref name="argus" />

== Teaching career ==

Noha has founded four Aikido dojos over his career:

* '''1970''' — Opened the first Aikido school in the [[Washington, D.C.]] area, in [[College Park,
  Maryland|College Park, Maryland]].<ref name="sensei" />
* '''1975''' — Established the first Aikido school in [[Buffalo, New York|Buffalo, New York]]. He
  also taught in [[Niagara Falls, Ontario]].<ref name="sensei" /><ref name="aj">{{cite web
  | url = https://aikidojournal.com/2020/06/09/aikido-as-an-art-of-personal-development-and-spiritual-growth-by-bob-noha/
  | title = Aikido as an Art of Personal Development and Spiritual Growth, by Bob Noha
  | website = Aikido Journal
  | access-date = 2026-09-08
}}</ref>
* '''1974–1975''' — Served as a Security Police Tactics instructor at [[Andrews Air Force Base]],
  teaching arrest/restraint techniques to U.S. Military Police.<ref name="sensei" />
* '''1982''' — Founded Aikido of Novato in [[Novato, California|Novato, California]].<ref name="sensei" />
* '''1983''' — Founded Aikido of Petaluma in [[Petaluma, California|Petaluma, California]], where he
  continues to serve as chief instructor.<ref name="sensei" />

Noha traveled to [[Japan]] to deepen his Aikido training in 1998, 1999, and 2006.<ref name="simonschuster" />

== Rank progression ==

According to his dojo's biography, Noha's rank progression in Aikido is:<ref name="sensei" />

* '''5th dan''' (''godan'') — awarded January 2000<ref name="sensei" />
* '''6th dan''' (''rokudan'') — awarded January 2013, with the recommendation of [[Robert Frager]]

* '''7th dan''' (''nanadan'') — awarded January 2025, confirmed by the [[Aikikai]] Hombu Dojo in the

  [[John Stevens (author)|John Stevens]]<ref name="aikikai-2025" /><ref name="sensei" />

== Cross-training ==

In addition to Aikido, Noha has been a devoted student and teacher of [[T'ai Chi Ch'uan|T'ai Chi]]
since 1972, studying the [[Yang style T'ai Chi Ch'uan|Yang short form]] from both [[Cheng
Man-Ch'ing|Professor Cheng Man-Ch'ing]] and [[Robert W. Smith (author)|Robert W. Smith]], a senior
student of Professor Cheng. He also has a background in [[Judo]], [[Kenpo|Kenpo Karate]], [[Pa Kua
Chang|Baguazhang]], [[Hsing-I|Xingyiquan]], and [[western boxing]].<ref name="sensei" />

== Philosophy and teaching approach ==

Noha's teaching emphasizes Aikido as a tool for personal development and spiritual growth, rather
than purely as a martial art. He has written about Aikido's potential to produce "profound spiritual
awakening" comparable to [[Zen]] and [[Yoga]], and advocates for specific practices that address
the "manifest/physical," "hidden/energetic," and "divine/emptiness" states of consciousness that
[[Morihei Ueshiba]] discussed in his lectures.<ref name="aj" />

He describes his approach as "gentle martial arts for the pressures of daily life" and has focused
on attracting students who identify as "spiritual, but not religious."<ref name="aj" />
