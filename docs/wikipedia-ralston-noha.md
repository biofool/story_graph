## Peter Ralston

{{Draft article|Peter Ralston (martial artist)}}
{{WikiProject Biography|living=yes|martial-arts=yes|aikido=yes}}

'''Peter Ralston''' is an American martial artist, consciousness teacher, and author. He is the
founder of the [[Cheng Hsin]] School of Internal Martial Arts and Center for Ontological Research,
based in [[Oakland, California]]. In 1978, Ralston became the first non-Asian to win the World
Championship full-contact [[martial arts]] tournament held in the [[Republic of China]]
([[Taiwan]]).<ref name="chenghsin-bio">{{cite web
  | url = https://chenghsin.com/who-is-peter-ralston/
  | title = Who is Peter Ralston?
  | publisher = Cheng Hsin
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

He is the author of several books on consciousness and martial arts, including ''The Book of Not
Knowing'' (2010), ''Zen Body-Being'' (2006), and ''Cheng Hsin: The Principles of Effortless Power''
(1989).<ref name="openlibrary">{{cite web
  | url = https://openlibrary.org/search.json?author=Peter+Ralston

== Early life and martial arts training ==

Ralston was born in [[San Francisco]], [[California]], but was raised primarily in [[Asia]]. He
began studying martial arts at the age of nine in [[Singapore]], starting with [[Judo]]. By the age
of twenty, he had earned [[black belt]]s in [[Judo]], [[Jujitsu]], and [[Karate]]. He was the [[Sumo]]
champion at his high school in [[Japan]] and the Judo and [[fencing]] champion at the [[University of
California, Berkeley|UC Berkeley]].<ref name="chenghsin-bio" />

His early training also included [[Kempo]], [[Ch'uan Fa]], and [[Northern Sil Lum Kung Fu]]. A
growing interest in [[internal martial arts]] led him to study [[T'ai Chi Ch'uan|T'ai Chi Ch'üan]],
[[Hsing I Ch'uan|Xingyiquan]], and [[Pa Kua Chang|Baguazhang]], as well as [[Aikido]], Japanese and
Chinese fencing, [[western boxing]], and [[Muay Thai]].<ref name="chenghsin-bio" />

== Cheng Hsin School ==

In 1975, Ralston founded the '''Cheng Hsin School''' ({{zh|c=誠心|p=Chéngxīn|l=sincere heart}}), a
martial arts and consciousness research school. In 1977, he opened a center called The Cheng Hsin
School of Internal Martial Arts and Center for Ontological Research in [[Oakland, California]]. The
school teaches the "Art of Effortless Power," an internal martial art that uses principles of
joining, complementing, and leading to develop interactive skills.<ref name="chenghsin-bio" />

Cheng Hsin has affiliated schools in [[Australia]], [[Germany]], [[France]], [[Hungary]], the
[[Netherlands]], [[South Korea]], [[Sweden]], and [[Wales]].<ref name="chenghsin-site">{{cite web
  | url = https://chenghsin.com
  | title = Cheng Hsin — Official Site
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

== 1978 World Championship ==

In 1978, Ralston became the first non-Asian ever to win the World Championship full-contact martial
arts tournament held in the Republic of China (Taiwan). This achievement is noted on his official
biography and in publisher profiles.<ref name="chenghsin-bio" /><ref name="penguin" />

== Consciousness work ==

Ralston is described as one of the founders of the [[consciousness movement]] that began in the
[[San Francisco Bay Area]] in the late 1960s and early 1970s. He worked with [[Stewart Emery]] in
[[Actualizations]] and helped [[Werner Erhard]] develop a shift in his work from [[EST]] to
developing [[The Forum]]. He spent thousands of hours in [[Zen]] contemplation and participated in
dozens of intensives with [[Charles Berner]], the founder of the [[Enlightenment
Intensive]].<ref name="chenghsin-bio" /><ref name="origins">{{cite web
  | url = https://chenghsin.com/origins-interview-with-peter-ralston/
  | title = Origins Interview with Peter Ralston
  | publisher = Cheng Hsin
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

After several enlightenment experiences in the early 1970s, Ralston's teaching approach has been to
lead students away from beliefs and toward direct personal experience and discovery. He has
facilitated workshops for [[Esalen]], Lifesprings, and [[Tony Robbins]]' NLP trainings.

## Bob Noha

{{Draft article|Bob Noha}}
{{WikiProject Biography|living=yes|martial-arts=yes|aikido=yes}}

'''Robert "Bob" Noha''' (born [[Chicago|Chicago, Illinois]]) is an American [[Aikido]] instructor
holding the rank of 7th ''[[dan (rank)|dan]]''. He is the founder and chief instructor of [[Aikido
of Petaluma]] in [[Petaluma, California|Petaluma, California]], which he established in 1983. Noha
has practiced Aikido since 1966 and is a lifelong student of [[Robert Nadeau (aikidoka)|Robert Nadeau
Shihan]], who was a direct student of [[Morihei Ueshiba]], the founder of Aikido.<ref name="sensei">{{cite web
  | url = https://www.aikidopetaluma.com/our-sensei/
  | title = Our Sensei — Robert "Bob" Noha
  | publisher = Aikido of Petaluma
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

He is the co-author of ''Aikido: The Art of Transformation — The Life and Teachings of Robert
Nadeau''.<ref name="simonschuster">{{cite web
  | url = https://www.simonandschuster.net/authors/Bob-Noha/221805037
  | title = Bob Noha — Simon & Schuster
  | publisher = Simon & Schuster
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

== Early life and training ==

Noha was born in [[Chicago]] to [[Czechoslovakia]]n-American parents. His father was a
parachutist with the U.S. Airborne forces during [[World War II]]. The family moved frequently and
eventually settled in [[Maryland]] near his father's military base.<ref name="argus">{{cite web
  | url = https://www.aikidopetaluma.com/2015/09/20/profile-robert-noha-article-from-the-argus-courier-may-14-2003/
  | title = Profile: Robert Noha — Argus Courier, May 14, 2003
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

Noha began practicing Aikido in 1966 at the Mountain View Aikikai in [[Mountain View, California]],
under [[Ed Riggs]] and [[Sig Kufferath]]. Shortly thereafter, he began training with [[Robert Nadeau
(aikidoka)|Robert Nadeau]], which started a lifelong friendship and teacher-student relationship.
His first martial arts training was in [[boxing]] and [[Kenpo]] [[Karate]] under Harvey Cleary, a
student of [[Ed Parker]].<ref name="maytt">{{cite web
  | url = https://maytt.home.blog/2021/02/17/interview-with-californian-aikidoka-robert-noha-aikidos-spiritual-aspect/
  | title = Interview with Californian Aikidoka Robert Noha: Aikido's Spiritual Aspect
  | publisher = Martial Arts of Yesterday, Today and Tomorrow
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>

Noha enrolled in the [[University of Maryland]], majoring in [[philosophy]], and served in the
[[United States Air Force|U.S. Air Force]] reserves.<ref name="argus" />

== Teaching career ==

Noha has founded four Aikido dojos over his career:

* '''1970''' — Opened the first Aikido school in the [[Washington, D.C.]] area, in [[College Park,
  Maryland|College Park, Maryland]].<ref name="sensei" />
* '''1975''' — Established the first Aikido school in [[Buffalo, New York|Buffalo, New York]]. He
  also taught in [[Niagara Falls, Ontario]].<ref name="sensei" /><ref name="aj">{{cite web
  | url = https://aikidojournal.com/2020/06/09/aikido-as-an-art-of-personal-development-and-spiritual-growth-by-bob-noha/
  | title = Aikido as an Art of Personal Development and Spiritual Growth, by Bob Noha
  | publisher = Aikido Journal
  | accessdate = 2025-09-08
}}</ref>
* '''1974–1975''' — Served as a Security Police Tactics instructor at [[Andrews Air Force Base]],
  teaching arrest/restraint techniques to U.S. Military Police.<ref name="sensei" />
* '''1982''' — Founded Aikido of Novato in [[Novato, California|Novato, California]].<ref name="sensei" />
* '''1983''' — Founded Aikido of Petaluma in [[Petaluma, California|Petaluma, California]], where he
  continues to serve as chief instructor.<ref name="sensei" />

Noha traveled to [[Japan]] to deepen his Aikido training in 1998, 1999, and 2006.<ref name="simonschuster" />

== Rank progression ==

Noha's rank progression in Aikido:

* '''5th dan''' (''godan'') — awarded January 2000<ref name="sensei" />
* '''6th dan''' (''rokudan'') — awarded January 2013, with the recommendation of [[Robert Frager]]
  and the late [[Hiroshi Kato]] Shihan<ref name="sensei" />
* '''7th dan''' (''nanadan'') — awarded January 2025, with recommendations from [[Bill Witt]] and
  [[John Stevens (author)|John Stevens]]<ref name="sensei" />

== Cross-training ==

In addition to Aikido, Noha has been a devoted student and teacher of [[T'ai Chi Ch'uan|T'ai Chi]]
since 1972, studying the [[Yang style T'ai Chi Ch'uan|Yang short form]] from both [[Cheng
Man-Ch'ing|Professor Cheng Man-Ch'ing]] and [[Robert W. Smith (author)|Robert W. Smith]], a senior
student of Professor Cheng. He also has a background in [[Judo]], [[Kenpo|Kenpo Karate]], [[Pa Kua
Chang|Baguazhang]], [[Hsing-I|Xingyiquan]], and [[western boxing]].<ref name="sensei" />

== Philosophy and teaching approach ==

Noha's teaching emphasizes Aikido as a tool for personal development and spiritual growth, rather
than purely as a martial art. He has written about Aikido's potential to produce "profound spiritual
awakening" comparable to [[Zen]] and [[Yoga]], and advocates for specific practices that address
the "manifest/physical," "hidden/energetic," and "divine/emptiness" states of consciousness that
[[Morihei Ueshiba]] discussed in his lectures.<ref name="aj" />

He describes his approach as "gentle martial arts for the pressures of daily life" and has focused
on attracting students who identify as "spiritual, but not religious."<ref name="aj" />
