'''Robert "Bob" Noha''' (born [[Chicago|Chicago, Illinois]]) is an American [[Aikido]] instructor
holding the rank of 7th ''[[dan (rank)|dan]]'', confirmed by the [[Aikikai]] Hombu Dojo in the 2025
[[Kagami Biraki]] promotion list.<ref name="aikikai-2025">{{cite web
  | url = https://aikikai.or.jp/pdf/suisen/2025.pdf
  | title = The list of Kagamibiraki Grading in 2025 (令和7年鏡開き推薦昇段者一覧)
  | publisher = Aikikai Foundation (公益財団法人合気会)
  | access-date = 2026-09-08
  | format = PDF
}}</ref> He is the founder and chief instructor of [[Aikido of Petaluma]] in [[Petaluma,
California|Petaluma, California]], which he established in 1983. Noha has practiced Aikido since 1966
and is a lifelong student of [[Robert Nadeau (aikidoka)|Robert Nadeau Shihan]], who was a direct
student of [[Morihei Ueshiba]], the founder of Aikido.<ref name="sensei">{{cite web

  | title = Our Sensei — Robert "Bob" Noha

  | access-date = 2026-09-08
}}</ref>

He is the co-author of ''Aikido: The Art of Transformation — The Life and Teachings of Robert
Nadeau''.<ref name="simonschuster">{{cite web

== Early life and training ==

Noha was born in [[Chicago]] to [[Czechoslovakia]]n-American parents. His father was a
parachutist with the U.S. Airborne forces during [[World War II]]. The family moved frequently and
eventually settled in [[Maryland]] near his father's military base.<ref name="argus">{{cite news
  | url = https://www.aikidopetaluma.com/2015/09/20/profile-robert-noha-article-from-the-argus-courier-may-14-2003/
  | title = Profile: Robert Noha
  | newspaper = Argus Courier
  | date = 2003-05-14
  | access-date = 2026-09-08
}}</ref>

Noha began practicing Aikido in 1966 at the Mountain View Aikikai in [[Mountain View, California]],
under [[Ed Riggs]] and [[Sig Kufferath]]. Shortly thereafter, he began training with [[Robert Nadeau
(aikidoka)|Robert Nadeau]], which started a lifelong friendship and teacher-student relationship.
His first martial arts training was in [[boxing]] and [[Kenpo]] [[Karate]] under Harvey Cleary, a
student of [[Ed Parker]].<ref name="maytt">{{cite interview
  | last = Noha
  | first = Robert
  | title = Interview with Californian Aikidoka Robert Noha: Aikido's Spiritual Aspect
  | work = Martial Arts of Yesterday, Today and Tomorrow
  | date = 2021-02-17
  | url = https://maytt.home.blog/2021/02/17/interview-with-californian-aikidoka-robert-noha-aikidos-spiritual-aspect/
  | access-date = 2026-09-08
}}</ref>

Noha enrolled in the [[University of Maryland]], majoring in [[philosophy]], and served in the
[[United States Air Force|U.S. Air Force]] reserves.<ref name="argus" />

== Teaching career ==

Noha has founded four Aikido dojos over his career:

* '''1970''' — Opened the first Aikido school in the [[Washington, D.C.]] area, in [[College Park,
  Maryland|College Park, Maryland]].<ref name="sensei" />
* '''1975''' — Established the first Aikido school in [[Buffalo, New York|Buffalo, New York]]. He
  also taught in [[Niagara Falls, Ontario]].<ref name="sensei" /><ref name="aj">{{cite web
  | url = https://aikidojournal.com/2020/06/09/aikido-as-an-art-of-personal-development-and-spiritual-growth-by-bob-noha/
  | title = Aikido as an Art of Personal Development and Spiritual Growth, by Bob Noha
  | website = Aikido Journal
  | access-date = 2026-09-08
}}</ref>
* '''1974–1975''' — Served as a Security Police Tactics instructor at [[Andrews Air Force Base]],
  teaching arrest/restraint techniques to U.S. Military Police.<ref name="sensei" />
* '''1982''' — Founded Aikido of Novato in [[Novato, California|Novato, California]].<ref name="sensei" />
* '''1983''' — Founded Aikido of Petaluma in [[Petaluma, California|Petaluma, California]], where he
  continues to serve as chief instructor.<ref name="sensei" />

Noha traveled to [[Japan]] to deepen his Aikido training in 1998, 1999, and 2006.<ref name="simonschuster" />

== Rank progression ==

According to his dojo's biography, Noha's rank progression in Aikido is:<ref name="sensei" />

* '''5th dan''' (''godan'') — awarded January 2000<ref name="sensei" />
* '''6th dan''' (''rokudan'') — awarded January 2013, with the recommendation of [[Robert Frager]]
  and the late [[Hiroshi Kato]] Shihan<ref name="sensei" />
* '''7th dan''' (''nanadan'') — awarded January 2025, confirmed by the [[Aikikai]] Hombu Dojo in the
  2025 [[Kagami Biraki]] promotion list, with recommendations from [[Bill Witt]] and
  [[John Stevens (author)|John Stevens]]<ref name="aikikai-2025" /><ref name="sensei" />

All Kagami Biraki promotions of 5th dan and above are recommendation ranks issued by the Doshu and
the Hombu Dojo Executive Committee.<ref name="caa-rank-info">{{cite web

  | title = Recommendation Rank Instructions
  | website = California Aikido Association
  | access-date = 2026-09-08
}}</ref>

== Cross-training ==

In addition to Aikido, Noha has been a devoted student and teacher of [[T'ai Chi Ch'uan|T'ai Chi]]
since 1972, studying the [[Yang style T'ai Chi Ch'uan|Yang short form]] from both [[Cheng
Man-Ch'ing|Professor Cheng Man-Ch'ing]] and [[Robert W. Smith (author)|Robert W. Smith]], a senior
student of Professor Cheng. He also has a background in [[Judo]], [[Kenpo|Kenpo Karate]], [[Pa Kua
Chang|Baguazhang]], [[Hsing-I|Xingyiquan]], and [[western boxing]].<ref name="sensei" />

== Philosophy and teaching approach ==

Noha's teaching emphasizes Aikido as a tool for personal development and spiritual growth, rather
than purely as a martial art. He has written about Aikido's potential to produce "profound spiritual
awakening" comparable to [[Zen]] and [[Yoga]], and advocates for specific practices that address
the "manifest/physical," "hidden/energetic," and "divine/emptiness" states of consciousness that
[[Morihei Ueshiba]] discussed in his lectures.<ref name="aj" />

He describes his approach as "gentle martial arts for the pressures of daily life" and has focused
on attracting students who identify as "spiritual, but not religious."<ref name="aj" />
