# Kufferath Notability — Phase I Source Search Results

**Subject:** Siegfried "Sig" Kufferath (1911-02-16 – 1999-05-07)
**Goal:** Identify independent, reliable, secondary sources that discuss Kufferath in meaningful depth, to support a standalone English Wikipedia biography under WP:GNG.
**Constraint:** No local library access. Free/open archives and on-wiki access requests (WP:RELIBRARY/WP:RX) only.
**Date of search:** 2025-01-15

## Executive summary

Phase I discovery found **five confirmed independent secondary newspaper sources** and **six strong paywalled or physical-access leads**. The confirmed sources span Kufferath's California teaching career (1961, 1966) and his death (1999):

**Confirmed independent sources (Tier A):**
1. **Los Gatos Times-Saratoga Observer, May 18, 1961, p.8** — a **dedicated profile** of Kufferath naming his rank (5th dan), Honolulu Police Department instruction, and California teaching. This is the strongest single source — substantive independent secondary coverage, not just an obituary.
2. **The Peninsula Times Tribune, June 1, 1966, p.49** — "Masters of aikido" **feature with photograph** naming Kufferath as head instructor of Nikko Jujitsu School in Mountain View.
3. **Honolulu Star-Bulletin, June 23, 1999** — obituary identifying him as "a jujitsu instructor" with "full military honors" at Punchbowl.
4. **Honolulu Star-Bulletin, May 14, 1999** — obituary confirming death, family, Dole occupation.
5. **Los Gatos Times-Saratoga Observer, May 11, 1961, p.1** — recreation board meeting coverage mentioning Kufferath's class request (brief, supports A3).

**Strong leads still requiring access (Tier B):**
1. A **Honolulu Advertiser article from October 16, 1943** that names Kufferath receiving an instructor diploma from Okazaki and entering military service — independent Honolulu newspaper coverage from his active martial-arts period.
2. The **Honolulu Advertiser obituary of Okazaki (July 13, 1951, p.12)** — a major Honolulu newspaper covering the death of Kufferath's teacher, which may name Kufferath as successor.
3. **Wally Jay's "Small-Circle Jujitsu"** (Black Belt Communications / Ohara Publications, 1989) — a book from an established martial-arts publisher, written by a fellow Okazaki student who founded a *different* system and organization. If it mentions Kufferath substantively, it is an independent secondary source.
4. **Honolulu newspaper sports coverage (1929–1932)** of the Hawaiian AAU 440-yard track championships, where Kufferath was reportedly a three-time champion — a completely independent angle from his martial-arts career.
5. A **San Jose Mercury News obituary (May 1999)** — a major California metro paper covering his death in Santa Clara.

All paywalled leads are flagged **CITATION PENDING — access needed** and should be requested via WP:RELIBRARY or WP:RX before citation. No unread source should be cited as evidence of notability.

The affiliated/organizational sources (danzan.com, kodenkan.com, AJJF, AJI, Kilohana, USAdojo, Ku'ialuaopuna mirrors, Tetsu-Tori, danzanryu blog, etc.) remain useful for factual verification but **cannot establish WP:GNG** because they lack independence from the subject's lineage.

## Assessment of notability gap after Phase I

### What Phase I established

1. **Five independent newspaper sources** now confirm Kufferath's martial-arts career across three decades:
   - **1961**: Los Gatos Times-Saratoga Observer ran a **dedicated profile** (A3) naming Kufferath, his 5th dan rank, his Honolulu Police Department instruction, and his California teaching — this is substantive "significant coverage" by Wikipedia standards.
   - **1966**: The Peninsula Times Tribune ran a **"Masters of aikido" feature with photograph** (A4) naming Kufferath as head instructor of Nikko Jujitsu School in Mountain View.
   - **1999**: Two Honolulu Star-Bulletin obituaries (A1, A2) confirm his death, martial-arts profession, and military service.
   - **1961**: Los Gatos Times-Saratoga Observer recreation board coverage (A5) mentions his class request.

2. **Six strong leads** remain that, if verified, could further strengthen the case:
   - Two Honolulu Advertiser articles (1943, 1951) — contemporaneous independent newspaper coverage from his active Honolulu period
   - Wally Jay's book (1989) — independent secondary source from an established publisher, if it mentions Kufferath
   - Honolulu newspaper sports coverage (1929–1932) — independent angle from his AAU track career
   - San Jose Mercury News obituary (1999) — major California metro paper
   - WWII Army enlistment record — primary but independent, confirms military service

### What Phase I did NOT establish

1. **No independent book, thesis, or scholarly article** discussing Kufferath in substantial depth was found in free-access channels. The books that exist (Arrington, Janovich) are self-published and affiliated. Wally Jay's book is the one exception that needs physical-access verification.

2. **No independent newspaper profile or feature article from his Honolulu years (1937–1957)** was found in free-access channels. The Honolulu Advertiser 1943 and 1951 articles are the most likely candidates but are paywalled.

3. **No academic or scholarly treatment** of Kufferath or Danzan-ryū history was found via Google Scholar. The field is under-researched academically.

### Revised assessment

The notability case is now **materially stronger** than after the initial web search. With five confirmed independent secondary sources — including a **dedicated 1961 newspaper profile** (A3) and a **1966 feature with photograph** (A4) — the article has substantive independent coverage spanning his California teaching career, not just brief obituary notices.

The 1961 profile (A3) is particularly significant: it is a dedicated article about Kufferath, not a passing mention, and it independently verifies his rank, Honolulu Police Department instruction, and teaching history. Combined with the 1966 feature (A4) and the two 1999 obituaries (A1, A2), this provides **multiple independent secondary sources** discussing Kufferath in meaningful depth across a 38-year span.

A WP:GNG argument now rests on:
- **A3 (1961 profile)** — significant coverage (dedicated profile article)
- **A4 (1966 feature)** — significant coverage (feature with photo)
- **A2 (1999 obituary)** — secondary coverage (obituary naming profession)
- **A1 (1999 obituary)** — secondary coverage (obituary)
- **A5 (1961 board meeting)** — supporting coverage

This is a **defensible GNG case** — multiple independent reliable sources providing significant coverage. The Tier B leads, if verified, would further strengthen the case but are no longer strictly necessary for a GNG argument.

**The remaining gap** is Honolulu-era independent coverage (1937–1957). The Honolulu Advertiser 1943 and 1951 articles (B1, B2) would fill this if verified, but the California-era coverage (A3, A4) already establishes notability for his post-1957 career.
