An adventurer’s tale of the high life

When noted Berkeley mountaineer Arlene
Blum returns to Sacramento next week, she'll be
talking and walking, not climbing.

Her slide-lecture program, titled “The Great Hi-
malayan Traverse,” will cover her nine-month,
2,000-mile traverse across the top of the world —
through Bhutan, Nepal and northern India. She
made the epic adventure with Hugh Swift, a veter-
an trek leader from Berkeley.

Their route, she says, led them “through a di-
versity of peoples, cultures, plants and animals
that is unsurpassed anywhere on this planet.”

The show takes place Thursday, Oct. 22, at 7:30
p.m. in the Sacramento City College auditorium,
3835 Freeport Blvd. Admission is $5

Sponsored by the Sierra Club's Mother Lode
Chapter, the 90-minute program will feature twin
projectors, local music taped during the trek and
Blum’s live narration.

Blum is widely known as the leader of the 1978
American Women's Himalayan Expedition,
which made history as the first Americans — and
the first women — to scale the perilous slopes of
Annapurna. However, the triumph soon became
tragedy when two climbers on the second summit
team fell to their deaths

That group also will be remembered for Blum's
compelling book: “Annapurna: A Woman's Place
published by Sierra Club Books. (In a previous
Sacramento visit, Blum presented a well-received
slide show on her Annapurna experiences.)

The Great Himalayan Traverse began near the
eastern border of Bhutan, “a smal! Buddhist king
dom virtually untouched by outside influence.’
Blum and Swift were the first Westerners to re-
ceive permission to walk across this remote land
