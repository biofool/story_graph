Ralston says. “Sure I could turn
out trained killers if I wanted to.
But it’s a rare thing to need self-
defense training. When was the
last time you were attacked for no
reason?

“We're teaching an art, a his-
tory, a tradition that will hopefully
improve people's lives. We're not
running a boxing school.”

Most well-run schools encour-
age beginners to start in group
classes averaging $25-to-$35 for
four weekly lessons. But some high
pressure outfits make admittance
contingent on five private lessons
at $19.50 each. After agreeing to
this introductory package, students
are pressured to contract for the
long haul.

At some schools free uniforms
are given to those who sign up at
the end of the fourth lesson. And
here is how one chain suggests to
its salespersons how to handle
questions about price:

“Until we've had a chance to
work with you, we don't know
how long it’s going to take. But I'll
give you an idea what the average
student pays. Let’s say a guy
comes in here with his arm in a
cast and black eye, he needs an
accelerated program. A guy like
this could pay as much as $20 a
week. (Pause) Or $14 a week. But
the average student pays $10 a
week.”

Resistance is met this way:
“Now we do have one problem
here. With the recent TV shows
and popularity of Kung Fu we're
filling up fast. We're making plans
now to open another school. Our
big problem is that we're getting
too many students, and can’t train
instructors fast enough. So. our
plans now are to cut off new
memberships until we can train
instructors and open another
school. So all I can suggest is to
take the five lessons while we ‘Still
have openings...”

Typically, payments are billed

ment plan learn from the sales
office: “This is the only way we

“It would be much better if
the state would license all the
martial arts academies. I'm all in
favor of regulation,” Campbell
says. “A bad judo teacher can be
just as dangerous as a bad doctor
or dentist.”

In Sacramento, the state office
of private post-secondary educa
tion says it has no plans to regu
late these academies. Like boxing
gyms, martial arts schools do not
fall under professional or vocation-
al licensing programs, Consumer
fraud specialists with state and
local agencies report they have
received only occasional com-
plaints from dissatisfied customers.

“A good school doesn’t expose
you to any risk until you are ready
for it,’ says Cheng Hsin student
Charles Hink. “I had a buddy who
went to a karate school and got
really torn up. Some nut was
showing off for his girlfriend.”
