California parks offer guided tours ©

If you've never explored California’s parklands
on a naturalist-led tour, you’re missing something.
Guided walks let you experience new places — or
revisit old ones — through the eyes of the experts.

State and national parks, of course, generally
offer full-blown schedules during the busy sum-
mer season. But many also conduct special activi-
ties in the springtime. Best yet, most of these
programs are free.

Times and dates vary, so it’s always a good idea
to call ahead for specifics. And, upon arrival,
check the information board or ask a ranger for

that weekend's lineup of events. A number of -

parks, incidentally, also offer junior ranger pro-
grams in which youngsters can learn about the
ways of the wilds.

Here’s a Northern California sampling:

@ Mount Diablo State Park (415) 837-2525: If
Diablo’s main peak seems higher than its 3,849-
foot elevation, it’s because it rises so abruptly
from the surrounding countryside in Contra Costa
County. April weekends on the mountain include
guided hikes on such topics as birds, wildflowers
and astronomy. Star parties also take place from
timetotime. —~

© Mount Tamalpais State Park (415) 388-2070:
This park in Marin County features a labyrinth of
hiking and riding trails stretching from the sea to
the top of Mount Tam, as it’s often called. Nature
hikes take place Saturday mornings. On one Sat-
urday each month near the full moon, there’s also
a moonlight walk. Ask about the monthly star
watches.

e@ Marin Headlands (415) 331-1540: The scenic
Marin Headlands once formed part of the Bay
Area’s coastal-defense system. It’s now a unit of
the Golden Gate National Recreation Area with a
unique place that lets you get away from it all
within sight of one of America’s great cities.

ture walks, history talks and special workshops
for all ages, including: “Flights of Spring,” “Up Hill
and Dale,” “Going Bunkers,” “Your Dog in the
Wild,” “Bobcats of Marin,” “Women Walking Safe-
ly in the Wild,” “Invent an Insect” and “Aquatic
Adventure.”

Of special interest is the old Point Bonita Light-
house (to reach it, you cross a swaying suspension
bridge high above the pounding surf). One-hour
guided tours take place weekend afternoons, with
several full-moon and sunset walks also offered
each month.

@ Mendocino Coast state parks (707) 937-5804:
Mendocino’s wild beauty, coupled with the low-
key, old-time charm of the area’s well-spaced vil-
lages, makes this a destination with a thoroughly
romantic appeal.

State parks bordering this stretch of shoreline
include MacKerricher, Russian Gulch, Manches-
ter and Van Damme. Campfire programs, sunset
tours and special events begin in April. While
you're in the area, stop in at the Ford House Visi-
tors Center at Mendocino Headlands State Park
(which surrounds the picturesque town of Mendo-
cino). Theme exhibits include sea mammals in
March, indigenous birds in April, wildflowers in
May and edible algae in June.

e Yosemite National Park (209) 372-0264: In
this land of polished domes, sheer walls and tow-
ering waterfalls, you'll find something going on
seven days a week. Indeed, you can choose froma
long list of guided walks, evening programs, photo
clinics and weekend seminars. Consult the Yo-
semite Guide (handed out free at the park) for
details.

@ Muir Woods National Monument (415) 388-

2595: Always green, cool and inviting, this Marin
County park protects a group of towering coastal]
redwoods.

On Saturdays and Sundays, rangers lead short,
introductory tours. Topics vary each week, rang-
ing from redwood ecology to area history. Special
events occur once in a while, too. Recent ones
have included “Moonlight Magic” and “Woods
Family Walk.” Rangers also loan “nature-discov-
ery” packs to children.

@ Point Lobos State Reserve (408) 624-4909:
This exciting mosaic of wave-sculpted rocks,
foaming surf, bold headlands and gnarled Monte-
rey cypresses lies just south of Carmel. Rangers
and docents lead twice-daily walks on a variety of
topics. Times vary, so call ahead. Otherwise, ask
at the entrance station.

e Jack London State Historic Park (707) 938-
5216, north of Sonoma: Here, at the adventure
writer’s restored retreat, the sprawling Beauty
Ranch, you can combine history with walking.
Guided tours take place each weekend.

© Sonoma Coast State Beach (707) 875-3483;
Miles of wave-washed beaches, secluded coves,
natural arches and sand dunes highlight this State
park between Bodega Bay and Jenner. At Bodega
Head, through April 3, rangers discuss whales,
And, on weekends through June, there’s a’ seal
watch at Goat Rock.

e Point Reyes National Seashore (415) 663-
1092: This Marin coast park offers not only tan-
colored cliffs, dense forests and wind-whipped
bluffs, but also such naturalist activities as geolo-
gy talks, tide pool walks and bird watches. There
are also occasional evening tours of the ligft-
house.
