Rule wardens
retain rights to
search warrants

ST. PAUL (AP) — Atty. Gen.
Douglas Head has ruled that
State conservation officers
(game wardens) retain authori-
ty to obtain search warrants
from justices of the peace des-
pite recent changes in state law.

Head said Thursday a 1963
law changing the method of is-
suing warrants and a 1969 law
increasing misdemeanor penal-
ties to $300 did not alter the
rights of wardens to obtain war-
rants from justice courts.

Since conservation officers
often work at night and in re-
mote areas, investigations would
be hampered if they were re-
stricted to getting warrants
from municipal courts, Head
said.

The opinion was requested by
W. J. Spooner, Itasca County at-
torney.

In another ruling, Head said
townships generally lack legal
authority to levy special assess-
ments to pay for municipal wa-
ter systems.

The opinion was sought by
Fayal Township, near Eveleth,
which is seeking to build a wa-
ter distribution system hooked
‘to the Eveleth municipal water
supply. Head said the city of
Eveleth could legally charge a
higher rate for water outside of
its boundaries than for custom-
ers within the city limits.
