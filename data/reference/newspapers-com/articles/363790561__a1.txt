The Center for Battered Women
starts a volunteer training pro-
gram today for those who want to
counsel battered women and chil-
dren of violent homes.

Center leaders say minority vo-
lunteers are needed, and they say
concerned men also are welcome.

The 20-hour training program
will be from 6:30 to 9 p.m, today,
Wednesday, Feb. 4 and Feb. 7; and
from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. Feb. 2 and 9.
Sessions meet at the United Way
office, 1905 Poquito St. (corner of

Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard,

and Poquito Street). Call 385-0620
to reserve a space for the training.

An evening school program

aimed at curbing Austin’s one-in-
four dropout rate begins
Wednesday.

The program, sponsored by the
Austin Independent School Dis-
trict, is open to anyone age 16 or
older who has dropped out of
school without receiving a high
school diploma. It will also serve
students who are enrolled in
school but lack enough credits to
graduate with their classmates.

The program will be from 5:30 to
8:30 p.m. Mondays and Wednes-
days; or from 5:30 to 8:30 p.m.
Tuesdays and Thursdays. Classes
will meet at W.R. Robbins Second-
ary School, 3908 Avenue B. The se-
mester ends May 14.

Former students ages 16 to 21
who are residents of AISD may at-
tend evening classes free of
charge. Day students, non-resident
students and those older than 21
will be charged tuition. For tuition
information, call Martin Bera,
principal, at 450-0392, or Gloria
Williams, director of senior high
programs and services, at 45l-
8411, Ext. 317.

Registration will be from 7 to 9
p.m. today and 6 to 9 p.m. Tuesday
at W.R. Robbins school.
