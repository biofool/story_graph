LOS ANGELES TIMES

may be overstating the case a tad,
but with the introduction of a
small menu of macrobiotic food at
all 31 of the Ritz-Carlton hotels,
the sprout has arrived.

Schulze, the top dog at Ritz-Carl-

“T got into it because I was ill,”
he said during a telephone inter-

And the good news spread — to
the executive chef, who liked the
way, for example, the Sesame
Whitefish with Ziti Pasta and
Garlic Chips was prepared. And
to the VP of food and beverage,
who lost 35 pounds.

There is no doubt that Schulze’s
enthusiasm helped to bring the
Kushi Institute in to train the Ritz
chefs in the ways of macrobiotic
preparation. Schulze, however,
sees it more as a progression.

“We have a responsibility to

create food for a cross-section of
people here at the hotel. Eleven
years ago we went to the Ameri-
can Heart Association to help us
create healthy foods to put on our
menu. I see this as the next step.”

Macrobiotics (from the Greek
“macro” and “bios,” meaning
great life) was popularized in this
country by George Ohsawa 20
years ago, but his work began in
Paris in the 1920s, where he
christened his teaching about nu-
trition and health “macrobiotic.”

The mantle was taken up after
his death by two students, Michio
and Aveline Kushi, who founded
the influential East-West Journal,
the Erewhon natural foods com-
pany and the Kushi Institute. The
institute, in the Berkshire Moun-
tains of Massachusetts, is in-
volved in an educational program
about macrobiotics.
