Hot spots

1. New Melones: Fishing is
good all around. Bass are coming
out of the shallow coves and pock-
ets, and it's best where there's a lit-
tle cloudy water. Best bets are
flipping brown Pig 'n’ Jigs or work-
ing spinner baits, crank baits or
worms during the day. Best worm is
a straight-tailed Green Weenie. In
the evening, they're using a No. 13
floating Rapala or a Rattlin’ Rogue.
Trout fishing is slowing down.
They're taking a few near the spill-
way, near Peoria Cove and off the
rocks and out from Carson Creek.
Top getter is a shad imitation with a
willow leaf spinner ahead of the bait,
with flasher blades optional. Catfish-
ing is good in the creek channels
with crawdads, liver or shad in 10 to
20 feet of water. Nights and early
mornings are best, and they're tak-
ing them up to 21 pounds. Bluegill
are hitting wax worms or crickets in
25 feet of water. 209-736-4333.

2. Bodega Bay: They're taking
limits of salmon four miles west of
the harbor mouth and making it
back to the dock by noon. The salm-
on are averaging 10 to 13 pounds,
and up to 24 pounds. Rock cod fish-
ing has also been successful. The
New Sea Angler reports taking full
limits of rock cod on the days it’s not
salmon fishing, with sacks averag-
ing 70 to 80 pounds. Rock codders
are bagging canaries, goldeneyes,
bocaccios and miscellaneous reds,
with quite a few ling cod. They're
fishing Cordell Banks unless the
wind forces them to head for Ft.
Ross, where the sacks are a little
lighter. 707-875-3495.

3. Middle Fork Feather Riv-
er: Those fishing opening day here
saw sunshine, snowfall and every-
thing in between. Those who came
prepared for inclement weather and
gutted it out also saw a lot of fish.
All evidence says that a warming
trend will trigger lots of bug activity,
which will turn on the fly fishing.
“The bugs want to come out, but the
wind's keeping them down,” a local
source said. The last few days, the
river was good from Graeagle down
to Nelson Creek. Top-producing
lures were Panther Martin and Vi-
brex. 283-2733.

Also rans

Local waters: The American
River is dead. Folsom Lake is slow.
And there's not much happening on
the Sacramento River. The only lo-
cal action is shad at Freeport. From
the west side of the river, bankers
are throwing shad darts with a %4-
ounce weight and retrieving it imme-
diately, with no drift. The flow in the
American River was turned up to
1,400 cubic feet per second Tues-
day night as part of an experimental
salmon release. The flow from Nim-
bus Dam is scheduled to be raised
to 3,500 cfs Thursday night. The
flush of fresh water should bring the
shad up the American River. Not
many stripers being taken out of the
Sacramento River from Rio Vista to
Colusa. 363-6885.

Black Butte Reservoir: Last
week, two guys spent a night fishing
in the willows and sacked 314 crap-
pie on 20 dozen minnows, supplant-
ed with jigs. They threw back an
additional 100 crappie weighing less
than a half-pound but did keep one
that was 3 pounds, 4 ounces. The
last few days, the fishing’s been up
and down with the fluctuations in
the weather, and the schools of
crappie are breaking up. Catching
good numbers of bass, all right
about keeper size, on surface lures.
865-2665.

Caples Lake: The lake thawed
four days ago, and trollers are
starting to take mackinaw up to 16
pounds. Most are trolling Rebels,
Rapalas or Flatfish. Some are using
lead-core, some aren't. 209-258-
7304.

Clear Lake: Strong winds,
which started Thursday and haven't
let up since, blew away some excel-
lent crappie and bass fishing. “Be-
tween the full moon and the wind, it
just really put the zonks on it,” a lo-
cal marina operator said. 707-998-
3780.

North state: They re taking a lot
of planters out of the Trinity River,
above the lake, on night crawlers
behind flashers, a variety of flashers
or an egg/marshmallow combina-
tion. Best success was near the riv-
er's tributaries. Out on Trinity Lake,
it was almost strictly bass, either
with plugs or salt and pepper Fat
Gitzits. Fish are running from keeper
size to 3 pounds. Seeing a few trout
on night crawlers or egg/marshmal-
low combos. Lewiston Lake was
very good over the weekend. They
were taking trout from eight to 14
inches on a variety of flies, from
Lakeview Terrace upstream to the
Trinity Dam. Inclement weather
made a bust out of the opener on
the upper Sacramento and Mc-
Cloud rivers. Bass fishing is still
tops at Shasta Lake, where they're
sinking minnows and grubs 20 to 25
feet. They're fishing the rocky points
and around structures. Bass are
running 12 inches to 3 pounds with
some throwbacks. The trout fishing
is slow, with what action there is
coming on minnows, Z-Rays or Ra-
palas under the Pit River bridge, at
Big and Little Backbone and the
snags in the Pit River Arm. 243-
2643.
