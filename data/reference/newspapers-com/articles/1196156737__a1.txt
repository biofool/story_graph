RECREATION ROUNDUP

Injured Tibbitts shines at Fencing Nationals

By MARK PATTON
NEWS-PRESS SENIOR WRITER

An injured thumb couldn't keep
Travis Tibbitts from getting his foot
into the door of fencing’s elite.

Tibbitts, 18, earned a Division A
rating after finishing seventh in the
Division 1A Men's Sabre at the USFA
Summer Nationals at the Anaheim
Convention Center.

He entered the competition witha
No. 38 seeding and bleak prospects
after spraining his thumb about six
weeks before the event.

“I wasn't able to practice before
the competition — I literally got in
two hours just before it started,”
said Tibbitts, a member of the Pre-
sidio Fencing Club. “I was alittle bit
worried about that, but I'd asked a
friend ifhe wanted to start running
with me.

“T was just counting on being in
better overall shape than every-
one else.”

Tibbitts, a recent San Marcos High
graduate who has been recruited
to fence for UC San Diego next fall,
became even more concerned after
warming up with a friend who had
finished in the top eight the previ-
ous year.

“He just wrecked me,” he said.
“But took my first pool bout 5-1 and
thought, ‘Wait a second -.. maybe I
can do this.’ It all just rolled for me
after that.”

Tibbitts went 4-1 in pool bouts to
improve his seeding to No. 16 forthe
elimination rounds. He won his next
two bouts, which included a 15-7 vic-
tory in the round of 32 over Drew
Dickinson, another UC San Diego
recruit.

“My soon-to-be coach was watch-
ing, and I definitely wanted to win
that one,” he said.

Tibbitts also won his sweet 16
bout, 15-10, over a fencer who had
just knocked off the top seed. He
then lost in the elite eight to Dan

GIRLS VOLLEYBALL

The Santa Barbara Volleyball
Club’s 18-Smack team placed 10th
in the 18-and-under open division
at the Junior National Girls Vol-
leyball Championships in Colum-
bus, Ohio. i

“Finishing in the top 10 is a great
accomplishment and something we
will all remember for a very long
time,” said coach Jason Donnelly,
who was assisted by Kat Niksto
and Tina Brown. “They practiced
at a really high level, fought hard
each and every time they stepped
on the court and continued to im-
prove all year long.

“They had to earn everything,
from a spot with Southern Califor-
nia’s elite teams to an at-large open
bid to the Junior Olympics.”

The team went 4-3 in pool play to
advance to the top 12. It then lost a
25-23, 25-23 playoff thriller to Sky
High 18-Black of Chicago.

It split its last two matches in the
bracket, beating Coast of Southern
California 25-20, 9-25, 15-12 before
falling to A-5 of Georgia 27-25, 23-

“Beating Coast on the last day
of JOs was particularly sweet as
we've had trouble with them this
year,’ Donnelly said.

The team consisted of nine col-

five from Dos Pueblos High: Sar-
ah McKinny (Vanguard), Amanda
Moriarty (Hawaii Pacific), Taylor
Racich (Pepperdine), Tristan Rac-

er (Hawaii). Two were from Santa

(Cal State Fullerton) and Dani Rott-

a senior next fall at San Marcos,
also played for 18-Smack.
