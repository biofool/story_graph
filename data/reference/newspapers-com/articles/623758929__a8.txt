Fall slow but pleasant at Ano Nuevo

Each winter at famed Ano Nuevo State Re-
serve, hundreds of elephant seals gather to breed.

And from December to April you can get sur-
prisingly close to these snouty, likable beasts —
assuming you're armed with a Ticketron reserva-
tion and accompanied by a tour guide.

Things slow down in autumn, however, and
you'll find few, if any, of the huge adult seals. Still,
it’s my favorite season at Ano Nuevo. Here’s why:

During fall, I know I can explore this hiker’s ha-
ven at my leisure, without booking in advance and
clinging to a group of camera-toting tourists. I'll
experience first-hand the park’s tide pools, sand
dunes, coastal lowlands and wind-whipped head-
lands. I'll also see lots of bird life and, if I’m lucky,
a few young elephant seals.

Foggy in summer and rainy in winter, Ano Nue-
vo, north of Santa Cruz off Highway 1, emerges
from the mist in fall.

“The off-season is a nice time to visit Ano Nue-
vo,” says a park docent. “You can take your time
and spend much of the day at the reserve. On the
winter tours, they march you out to look at the
seals and then march you right back again.”

From the visitor’s center, it’s a three-mile,
round-trip walk across the dunes and bluffs to the
seal area. There, naturalists are on hand daily
through November to answer questions and share
stories. You're also free to wander around the rest
of this beautiful, day-use-only reserve.

Ano Nuevo Island (off-limits) is visible from
many spots on the mainland. The metal tower of
the abandoned lighthouse has toppled, and bark-
ing sea lions have taken over the once-stately
keeper’s mansion, These fun-loving animals, do-
cents will tell you, sleep in the bathroom, climb
the stairs and peek out the second-floor windows.

On your walk, be sure to pack water, since
there’s none available on the trail. You'll be glad,
too, that you brought a jacket to ward off the wind

In the off-season, permits (free, but the park's
entrance fee is $3) are given out at the visitors
center on a first-come basis. During October,
crowds usually pose no problem. Sometime in No-
vember, the adult elephant seals begin to arrive
and so do the tourists. Get there early.

Officials close off the park’s seal area from
Dec. 1 to Dec. 11. Starting Dec. 12, you’ll need res-
ervations for one of the popular, 2.5-hour tours of-
fered daily through April 30. The $4 tickets go on
sale beginning Nov. 4 through Ticketron.

For more Ano Nuevo details, phone (415) 879-
0595.

Twenty-five miles north of Ano Nuevo, Half
Moon Bay State Beach (415-726-6238) offers
RV/tent camping for $10. You can book sites
through Mistix until Oct. 30, then it’s first-come.
The San Mateo Coast also includes Pigeon Point
Lighthouse (Sunday tours and a hostel, 415-879-
0633), day-use state beaches and quaint coastal
villages.

A final note: Half Moon Bay’s popular Pumpkin
Festival runs Oct. 17-18. Avoid the masses by visit-
ing earlier or later.

Exploring Tahoe — Wilderness Press, now
in its 20th year, produces the most up-to-date,
comprehensive guidebooks to California’s back-
country. And “The Tahoe Sierra” (by Jeffrey P.
Schaffer; $15.95) is one of the Berkeley publish-
er’s best.

This revised third edition covers 106 northern
Sierra hikes (from day walks to backpack treks)
and provides all the essential details for getting
there and being there.

Hikes are handily arranged according to geo-
graphical region. Among the areas described are
the new Granite Chief Wilderness, the expanded’
Mokelumne Wilderness and the under-construc-
tion Tahoe Rim Trail. |

In addition, “The Tahoe Sierra” includes sec-
tions of topographic maps — all corrected ver-
sions of the U.S. Geological Survey — keyed to
each hike. Author-map maker Schaffer, says the
publisher,.counts paces and checks his compass
constantly as he walks every trail. His work is‘said
to exceed government map-accuracy standards.
