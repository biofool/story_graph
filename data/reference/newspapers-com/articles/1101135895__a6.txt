Human Services Anniversary

POINT REYES — On
Tuesday, September 13, the
staff of West Marin Human
Services in Point Reyes Sta-
tion is holding an Open
House for the public from 2
pm to 6 pm to celebrate their
10th anniversary. The Center
was the first county facility
to provide integrated serv-
ices for the four major disci-
plines of Health and Human
Services.

The staff at West Marin
Human Services work on a
team concept to provide Pub-
lic Health, Community Men-
tal Health, Income Main-
tenance, and Social Services

to the residents of West
Marin. The territory served‘
starts with the communities
of Stinson Beach/Bolinas,
north to Tomales/Dillon
Beach, and -as far east as
White’s Hill, including the
four towns in the San Geroni-
mo Valley, for a total of 12
communities.

Approximately S00 to
600 individuals receive
services monthly by the

current staff of 22 professio-
nals. All of the staff will be
available at Tuesday’s cele-
bration which is being hosted
by Avis Drieschman, Assis-
tant Manager, who has been

with the Center from its start
in 1973.

In June 1982, Dennis
Scremin, who has been with
the Center since 1974, took
over the Center Manager
position from Diane Gara-
venta, who is now directing
Disaster Preparedness/
Services for Community
Mental Health at Green-
brae. The staff of West
Marin Human Services feels
they owe their success to
the help and friendship of
the people of West Marin
and are looking forward to
their celebration on Tuesday.
The public is invited to
attend. ©

Dance Palace classes showcased

POINT REYES — On
Saturday, September 10, at
8 p.m., the Dance Palace
will showcase its fall session
of classes with presentations
by teachers and students.
The free evening’s enter-
tainment includes tap dan-
cing by students of Molly
White, a demonstration of
Tai Chi Chu’an by Ellen
Serber-Estrin and her stu-
dents, and a brief film by
Zea Morvitz’s animation

class for kids.

Nancy Hemmingway
will demonstrate some exer-
cises for flexibility and _fit-
ness from her “Stretch and
Strengthen” class, and
dancers Melinda Leithold
and Lynn Hassan will contri-
bute “Shaking Loose” to
premiere their new class by
the same name. Murals
drawn in artist Mary Eu-
bank’s drawing class will
adorn the walls.

Other activities will in-
clude an Aikido demonstra-
tion by Richard Moon, David
Gamble and Sandy Jacobs,
and a yoga demonstration
with David Clarkson.

All Dance Palace
teachers will be available to
talk further about their
classes after the demonstra-
tions. The admission is free
amd refreshments will be
served.

Country dance coming

POINT REYES — A
country dance will be held at
the Dance Palace in Point
Reyes Station on Friday,
September 9, from 8 to 11
p.m.,

Caller Charlie Fenton
will lead circle, contra, mixer
and square dances. Live

Music provided by Cathie
Whitesides, fiddle, and Pete
Spicer, piano — two of the
finest dance musicians on the
West Coast.

Last month’s dance at
the Dance Palace drew a
large and enthusiastic crowd.
The hall resounded with live-

ly fiddling, clapping hands
and dancing feet. Delicious
refreshments will again be
provided by S.T.E.P.S.

All the dances are
taught on the spot, so be-
ginners are always welcome.
Come alone, or bring friends.
Admission is $3.50.

PTA plans
membership
campaign

WEST MARIN — The
National P.T.A. has pro-
claimed September as Mem-
bership Month. The West
Marin P.T.A., celebrating
its 1st anniversary, is join-
ing in the effort to increase
its membership.

Information, including
membership envelopes, will
be sent home with the
students during the first
week of classes. A special
reward will be presented to

the class returning the
most envelopes.
Membership in the local

P.T.A. also includes being a
part of the State and National
organizations which have
worked for 86 years as advo-
cates for the youth of our
nation. The programs in-
clude child safety, family
life, drug and alcohol abuse,

The local P.T.A. has,
this past year, staffed the art
room two days a week with
volunteers. Much of its
energy this year will be going
to an increased commitment
to the art room and a drive to
establish a computer center
at the school.

Residents of the commu-
nity are urged to join the
local P.T.A. even if they
don’t have children presently
enrolled in our schools. In
this way the community can
help work toward support-
ing, maintaining and im-
proving the education of our
future citizens.

Dues are $3 a year and
the first meeting will be Sep-
tember 12, 7:30 at the West
Marin School.
