John Kaminski’s Column

Some New Games Are Needed

We need new games.

We need them for two
reasons: because the tradi-
tional games teach us val-
ues that are dated, maybe
even obsolescent; and be-
cause there is so much
more to gain from our bod-
ies than a superficial sense
of physical well-being, the
primary utilization of which
is to be better than some- ‘
one else.

Now, the Esalen Sports
Center offers to anyone in-
terested the chance to gain
new perspectives, new ideas
for physical education, and
learn new games.

Located in California and
staffed by some of the
more _ forward thinking
minds and bodies in sport,
the Esalen Sports Center
will offer an intensive, two-
week program at Domini-
can, College in San Rafael,
California.

This program will present
many disciplines being
practiced at Esalen and
elsewhere as well as many
innovative approaches cur-
rently taking place in physi-
cal education.

The stated objective of the
seminar will be to find
points of integration be-
tween these newer disci-
plines and current methods
of athletic practice, coach-
ing, and physical education
instruction.

The program is designed
to provide brief but inten-
sive exposure to such areas
as:

Yoga.

The Programs

T’ai Chi is a way of life,
an East-West synthesis of
creative energy. In this
workshop participants will
explore the ‘‘Tao”’ of every-
day movement, the hoys of
sensing-feeling in the art of
simply moving and joining
the flow of the dance.

The tennis flow workshop
will integrate principles of
body awareness, movement,
dance, music and medita-
tion with traditional meth-
ods of tennis instruction
and practice, to master
flowing tennis strokes.

Golf and Energy Aware-
ness will be conducted by
Michael Murphy, author of
Golf in the Kingdom.

Centering and energy
awareness will be applied to
the game of golf. These
principles of mind-body har-
mony are derived from
meditation and aikido.

The Way of Hiking will
