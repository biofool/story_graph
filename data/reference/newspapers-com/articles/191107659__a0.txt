Star Tribune/Tuesday/October 25/1988

There may be no limit to what Carleton runner Prineas can do ;

By Jon Roe
Staff Writer

Atha Prineas can hardly wait to see
what she'll do next while running.

Maybe she'll think of a theme for a
new poem. Maybe she'll get an idea
for another short story. Or maybe
she'll win another cross-country race.

She already has won a fellowship for
her efforts in poetry. One of her short
stories has won a school award for
fiction writing. And this fall, the
Carleton College senior has run in
five cross-country meets and has tak-
en first place in all five.

Not bad for somebody who has been
participating in the sport for just two
years. Not bad at all for somebody
who hadn’t run farther than 800 me-
ters in competition until after her
sophomore year in college. ;

Now Prineas is the MIAC champion
indoors at 1,500 and 3,000 meters.
Shé is the conference champion out-
doors at 3,000, 5,000, and 10,000
meters. She is an NCAA Division III
cross-country All-America after fin-

Small colleges

ishing seventh in the national meet a
year ago. She won the Division III
indoor 3,000-meter title last spring,
and was second and fourth, respec-
tively, in the 10,000 and 5,000 at the
Division III nationals.

It’s happened so fast, even Prineas
doesn’t know what she can accom-
plish in running. “It’s all a new thing
for me,” she said. “It’s like I’m only
just getting started.”

If this is only a start, the future’

would seem to be limitless.

“Maybe she’ll be a Rhodes Scholar,”
said John Pfeiffenberger, coach of the
Carleton women’s track and cross-
country teams. “Maybe she'll be a
college professor. As a runner, who
knows? In just two years, she’s be-
come All-America in cross country
and in track. That tells you she has
done a whole lot of work and also
would seem to have a whole lot of
potential. She’s just starting to find
out what she can do, but each time
she tries something new, it’s like

she’s saying, ‘By golly, I think I can
do that.’”

What Prineas would like to do in the
immediate future is win the MIAC
women’s cross-country title Saturday
at St. Olaf. She was third a year ago
and has beaten the major challengers
in invitational meets this season. In
winning the University of Minnesota
Invitational recently, she even de-
feated NCAA Division I runners.

Prineas had been a soccer player after
her family moved from Australia to
Wayzata when she was six. But be-
fore her junior year at Carleton, she
and her younger brother, John, a
Carleton sophomore, decided to en-
ter Grandma’s Marathon. Later that
summer, Prineas ran in the Twin
Cities Marathon and won the 20-and-
under age category.

“It’s hard to tell someone why you
fall in love with running,” she said.
“But I have learned to love running.
Part of it is just the competition. The
competition builds confidence, the
confidence to try to accomplish
something new.”

However, Prineas has learned that
some things come slowly. She was
discovered to be dyslexic when she
was in second grade. Reading came
slowly and spelling was difficult.

“My spelling is still atrocious,” she
said. “But that’s what we have com-
puters for, isn’t it?”

Still, Prineas has maintained a 3.6
grade-point average at Carleton and
plans to attend graduate school, fo-
cusing on English literature and cre-
ative writing.

But there’s also running.

“I'm. still experimenting, I’m. still
learning,” she said. “But I feel that
the best years are still ahead. The
only thing I’m sorry about is that I
didn’t get started sooner.”

She is quickly making up for last
time, however.
