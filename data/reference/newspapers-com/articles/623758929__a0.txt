MORNING

FISHING

leans up to Somesbar. Salmon are
averaging 12 to 14 pounds, with
the biggest weighing in at 40
pounds. Best fishing seems to be
from the mouth of the Salmon River
up to Roger's Creek and Green's
Riffles. The fish are in good shape,
not beat up and not muddied. Wa-
ter is slow but looks good. Best bet
is a No. 9 to No. 15 Panther Martin,
black with yellow dots, perhaps
with a piece of red yarn on the end.
469-3350.

Lewiston Lake: Several small
rainbows, with 15 inches the big-
gest, are being taken. The big fish
are still laying low. A change in the
weather should trigger a feed.
Flashers and night crawlers are the
best bet followed by red salmon
eggs and marshmallows and then
the small and flashy stuff. The air is
clean and the weather warm.
They're picking up some clean
salmon, maybe half-a-fish per rod,
in the Trinity River up to Junction
City. Farther down the river some
guys are reporting no action while
others are taking limits. Word is
several steelhead are showing.
778-3770.

Modoc County: Fishing condi-
tions are still poor. Most of the res-
ervoirs are either weeded up or
dried out. Clear Lake and Blue
Lake are the only waters still pro-
ducing fish. Trollers are dragging a
night crawler behind blades in the
early morning and late evening for
small rainbows. Bait fishermen are
occasionally hooking bigger
browns bait-fishing 50 to 60 feet
down, 233-2423.

Sacramento River: Striper
fishing around Rio Vista (707-374-
5554) was good last week but died
down over the weekend. They're
still hooking a few on fresh and fro-
zen shad. Salmon fishing has
picked up, with several limits re-
ported at the beginning of the
week. They're trolling an Apex or a
silver K-16 Kwikfish around Decker
Island and above the Three-mile
Slough Bridge. On the upper Sac-
ramento River (243-2643), trout
fishing is good using weighted flies
for rainbows up to 16 inches, with
an occasional 18-incher. Best flies
are yellow jackets and black gnats.
Dry fly fishing is spotty. The release
at Keswick Dam as of Tuesday was
6,000 cfs. The fish count at the Red
Bluff diversion dam for a three-day
period ending Sunday was 4,479
salmon and 65 steelhead. Above
the dam, they’re averaging a fish
per rod using a silver T-50 or T-55
Flatfish with a sardine wrap. The
fish are running up to 30 pounds,
with several jacks showing from the
Deschutes Bridge south. The Los
Molinos area is producing limits
with fish averaging 22 to 25
pounds.
