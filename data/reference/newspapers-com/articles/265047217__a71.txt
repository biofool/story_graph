Roger Maltbie lost his lead in the second
round of the PGA Disney Classic golf
tournament yesterday at Lake Buena Vista, Fla.
It wasn't all that bad for him, though, since
Maltbie had just found out he still has his
home,

Maltbie, relieved to learn his home near San
Francisco survived Tuesday's earthquake, shot
a personal-best 9-under-par 63 for the lowest
score of the second round of the 72-hole
tournament being played on three different
courses,

Maltbie’s 10-under 134 total was good for a
share of the lead until late in the day, when
Tim Simpson overcame a rapid drop in
temperature and shot 67— 132 for a two-stroke
lead.

“Under the conditions, I was very pleased to
shoot five under,” Simpson said. “I think it
played several shots harder this afternoon than
it did this morning.”

Dave Rummells (68) and Ted Schulz (69)
were tied with Maltbie, while Bob Gilder,
Kenny Knox and Fred Couples each shot 65 to
lead a pack of eight at 135.

Bob Tway, who tied a tournament record
with a 61 in Wednesday's first round, followed
with a 77 to turn a four-stroke lead into a
six-stroke deficit.

“That's what keeps Excedrin in business,”

Simpson said.
Atlantic City fight night: Former
heavyweight champion Tim Witherspoon raised

his record to 30-3 by stopping Anders Eklund of
Sweden at 1:11 of the first round of a scheduled

10-rounder at Atlantic City, N.J.
Witherspoon dropped Eklund with a right
hook to record the 20th knockout of his career.
In a scheduled 10-round cruiserweight bout,
Jeff Lampkin of Youngstown, Ohio, stopped Ed
Smith of Gary, Ind., at 1:50 of the ninth round.
Heavyweight Charles Dread of Laurel, Md.,
was hospitalized in “critical but stable”
condition at the Atlantic City Medical Center
after being knocked out by Halken Brock.

Two 1988 U.S. Olympians scored first-round
knockouts in scheduled six-rounders.

Heavyweight Riddick Bowe of Brooklyn,
N.Y., raised his record to 9-0 when he scored
his eighth knockout, this one over Mike Acey
of Brisbin, Pa., and Anthony Hembrick of
Detroit improved to 8-0 by stopping Manuel
Muriello of Miami.
