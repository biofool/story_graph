The magical spell of the Sutter Buttes

It’s been a decade since Walt Anderson first
saw the Sutter Buttes, those bizarre crags and
peaks that tower above the otherwise flat Sacra-
mento Valley west of Yuba City.

“Little did I realize at the time the magnetic
hold and magical spell that the range would cast
upon me and upon thousands of visitors since
then,” Anderson wrote recently. “Long a sacred
range to the Indians who lived within sight of the
Buttes, it continues to draw and entice many who
view it.”

The only hitch, however, is that it’s all private
property. Fortunately, several naturalists, includ-
ing Anderson, have contracted with area ranch-
ers to take visitors on tours through this
mysterious inland island of grasslands, wood-
lands, jagged spires, rocky canyons and, in spring-
time, colorful fields of wildflowers. It’s a unique
concept of public use of private land.

Anderson describes the Buttes as “a feast for all
the senses.” Indeed, consider these intriguing ti-
tles of trips conducted by the Sutter Buttes Natu-
ralists: “The Heart of the People,” “A Flower of
Stone,” “A Day in the Sky,” “Gentle Slopes and
Quiet Beauty,” “The Mountain in Our Midst” and
“The Center of the World.”

Although volcanic action that lasted for a mil-
lion years formed the Buttes, Indian legends pro-
vide far more interesting explanations. For
example:

e “The Indian Creator had just finished mold-
ing the Sierra Nevada and Coast Range when, be-
ing very satisfied with his work, he dusted his
hands off. That dust fell down to the Sacramento
Valley to form the Buttes.”

e “An immense and beautiful tortoise, in her
desire to keep all at peace with one another,
raised the Buttes in the plain in order to separate
contending Indian tribes who were about to make
war upon one another, and thus prevent blood-
shed. No warrior dared attempt the crossing of
the mountains, as the good spirit of the peace-lov-

Over the years, lively debates over the Buttes
have taken place — with proposals ranging from
turning them into a state park to using them as a
storage area for nuclear waste. Those two propo-
sitions, like others, never got beyond the planning
stage.

From time to time, various groups (such as the
UC Davis Extension) sponsor field trips to the Sut-
ter Buttes. But the following organizations offer
regular tours:

¢ West Butte Sanctuary Co. leads Saturday trips
through the spring in the West Butte area. Cost
$20; half price for children 15 and under. A Sun-
day nature photography outing ($40) is offered
once a month through May. Contact and guide is
Allan Sartain, 1637 Baywood Lane, Davis 95616;
(916) 756-6283 (call after 4 p.m.).

© Sutter Buttes Naturalists, which includes Walt
Anderson, conduct day hikes every Saturday, and
some Sundays, in various parts of the Buttes. Cost
is $25; $15 for children ages 6 through 13; free for
5 and under. Group rates are available. Coordina-
tor is Ira Heinrich, 1128 Valley Oak Drive, Chico
95926; (916) 343-6614.
