Seniors bowl well on Oahu

On June 17-19 at the Schofield Bowling Center, 100 senior cit-
izens from the Parks and Recreation leagues of Hilo and Kona par-
ticipated in the State Senior Citizens Tournament on Oahu.

This tournament was coordinated for anyone 55 years and old-
er by the City and County of Honolulu, Parks and Recreation had
320 bowlers vying for top honors.

The two-day tournament was capped by an awards luncheon
held at the Ala Moana banquet hall. Daily prizes, division and age
awards and Island and Overall champions were honored at the ban-
quet.

Dolphin Golf Tourney results

The 4th annual Big Island Dolphin Golf Tournament benefit-
ing Hawaii Shriners Hospital for Children and the Pacific Marine
Life Foundation was held June 27 at Waikoloa Beach Course.

The soldout tournament offered prizes including $10,000 for
a hole-in-one sponsored by SmartMart (hole No. 17), a Honda and
round-trip air on American Airlines sponsored by Big Island
Honda (hole No. 13), a $20,000 shopping Spree and seven-day
Caribbean Cruise sponsored by Kings’ Shops (hole pas 11) and
a set of Ram golf Clubs (hole No. 7).

The winning team with a net of 54 on the par 70 course
included members Dan Hollembaek, Mike Murray, Jerry Glover,
Ralph Mendonsa and Pat Smith. Winners received a two-night stay
at Hilton Waikoloa Village, two rounds of golf for two at
Waikoloa’s Beach or Kings’ Course and a putter from Golf Gear.

Waiakea Nakoa’s Pop Warner
’ The Waiakea Nakoa’s Pop Warner Football Association will
hold its second football registration from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. Sat-
urday, July 11 at Panaewa Park.

Registration fee is $65 and due at that time.

The association will also hold low-cost physical exams for play-
ers at 9 a.m. Saturday, July 18 at Panaewa Park. Cost is $25.

A general membership meeting will follow at 9:30 a.m. Two
fundraiser car washes are scheduled, the first on July 25, from 10
a.m. to 2 p.m. at Walmart. That day will also be the final oppor-
tunity to register with the Nakoas’s.

For more information, call 935-5628.

Puna AYSO Soccer sign-up

Due to many requests, PUNA AYSO will continue its $40 reg-
istration fee. However, there will be no allowances for additional
players in the same family.

The soccer season starts at the end of August, and team sizes
are limited. All children 4 1/2 to 18 years old are encouraged to
play, a spokesperson said.

For more information or to register, call 966-8947.

Pearl City Aikido Club seminar

Pearl City Aikido Club will host a traditional Aikido summer
seminar in August on the Big Island of Hawaii.

All Aikido practitioners, regardless of style and affiliation, are
encouraged to attend this free seminar which will be held at the
Waiakea Rec. Center (located on Kamehameha Highway next to
KK Tei Japanese Restaurant) in Hilo.

The seminar will commence with an evening class on Friday,
Aug. 7 from 7 to 9 p.m. with three training sessions throughout
the day on Saturday, Aug. 8, from 8 to 10 a.m., 2 to 4 p.m. and
7 to 9 p.m. Traditional taijutsu (empty hand) and weapon tech-
nique will be covered extensively at the seminar.

Classes will be taught by Shihan Donald S. Moriyama, a 7th
degree black belt and Chief Instructor of the Pearl City Aikido Club
on Oahu. Moriyama Sensei has trained in Aikido for nearly 50 years
under Kisshomaru Ueshiba, Koichi Tohei and Morihiro Saito Sen-
seis. Moriyama Sensei is certified to teach Iwama-style weapons
including the jo (wooden staff) and bokken (sword).

“Our goal is to bring people together from different clubs for
spirited training in the tradition of Aikido founder Morihei
Ueshiba,” said Moriyama Sensei. “We look forward to further devel-
oping a camaraderie among students and instructors from the dif-
ficult clubs.”

For more information on the free seminar, call (808) 455-7000
on Tuesday or Thursday between the hours of 5:30 and 8 p.m. or
write to the Pearl City Aikido club at P.O. Box 1567, Pearl City,
HI. 96782.
