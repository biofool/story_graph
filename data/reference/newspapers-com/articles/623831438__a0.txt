Feather River: Stream fishing
in the Sierra closes Nov. 15, so as
long the weather is mild it might be a
good chance to take one last shot at
it. The middle fork of the Feather
River, from Sloat all the way down,
is still producing fish. Further up,
they're still taking some nice rain-
bows at Davis Lake. Look for deep
water along the shoreline or rocky
points and float a night-crawler.
Caribou Lake is producing trout up
to 3% pounds, when the power-
house is running early in the morn-
ing, on white crappie jigs and night-
crawlers. 283-2733.

Eagle Lake: The fishing was
slow before the full moon came
around, so after the fish have been
feeding all night one can imagine
how slow the action is. The fish they
are taking are nice — up to 6 or 7
pounds — but they're working hard
to get them. Bobber fishing in the
weeds and tules with a night-crawler
or trolling a blade and night-crawler
10 feet down between Rocky Point
and Pelican Point are the best bets.
825-2191.

Clear Lake: Kind of difficult to
get an accurate report because the
lake is off-limit to many of the lake's
regular fishermen, who will be par-
ticipating in the West Coast Bass
Tournament this weekend. The re-
ports filtering through, though, indi-
cate that clear water and the full
moon will make bass fishermen
work for their stringers. Experiment
until you find the right color. Piers
and docks might be the best bet.
Catfishing is good on stinky bait.
One fellow landed a 22%2-pound
channel cat last weekend in the
Oaks Arm with an anchovy. 998-
3780.

Lewiston Lake: The fishing’s
fair to good and on the upswing.
Temperatures are on the down-
swing, so bring warm gear. Trolling
Flatfish or drifting a Woolly Worm is
producing rainbows 14 to 16 inches.
An occasional bigger fish is starting
to show. 778-3770.

Sacramento River: Up Los
Molinos/Red Bluff way, salmon and
steelhead fishing is good to excel-
lent. They're bumping roe or back-
trolling a big Flatfish or Kwikfish with
a sardine wrap. Salmon are running
from 15 to 30 pounds. Steelhead to
8 pounds are taking GloBugs off the
riffles. 243-2643. Near Rio Vista
(707) 374-5554, the striper action is
picking up. Schoolies are ranging
from 5 to 18 pounds, averaging 15
pounds. Most are being taken on
mud suckers, while a few are hitting
on shad or Bombers. Best areas are
Decker Island to the Isleton Bridge
and Three-Mile Slough. They're see-
ing an occasional sturgeon on mud
suckers, shad and ghost shrimp
near Cache Slough.
