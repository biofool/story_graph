Fly So Free takes
on Derby history

By Joseph Durso
New York Times

Meadow Star won’t be there, trying
to become the fourth filly in 117
years to win the Kentucky Derby.

Dinard won't be there, trying to ex-
tend the recent success of horses
from California.

Cahill Road won’t be there, trying to
make history by winning the big race
the year after it was won by his full
brother, Unbridled.

But as the clans of racing gather at
Churchill Downs for the Run for the
Roses on Saturday, the favorites and
champions still are surrounded by
long odds. ;

Mostly, they are surrounded by the
odds of history: the traditions, super-
Stitions, jinxes, dosage index num-
bers and all the other dark influences
that foreshadow upsets and reversals
at Louisville.

And the horse bucking the most per-
sistent odds of recent history proba-
bly is the favorite, Fly So Free.

He is facing the fact that no 2-year-
old champion has gorie on to win the
Kentucky Derby as a 3-year-old since
Spectacular Bid did it in 1979.

Six of the dozen juvenile champions
since then were injured and missed
the chance.

Four of them broke down in succes-
sive years in the early 1980's: Lord
Avie, Deputy Minister, Roving Boy
and Devil’s Bag. Later in the decade,
Tasso broke down, too, and then
Rhythm didn’t run last year because
he had undergone surgery.

Most of the others stayed healthy but
not precocious enough to stay at the
head of the class at the age of 3.
Rockhill Native wasn’t, and neither
were Chief's Crown or Capote.

In fact, only two juvenile champions
came close to winning the Derby the
next season: Forty-Niner ran second

in 1988 and Easy Goer also ran sec-
ond in 89.

Otherwise, Fly So Free can look back
to Spectacular Bid as the last of the
bunch of 2-year-old valedictorians
who won the big one at age 3.

“T know that many people are skepti-
cal that he can keep winning,” said
Scotty Schulhofer, who trains the colt
for Thomas Valando of New York
City. “They say he loafs in the home-
stretch. They say he makes it close,
and one day he'll make it too close.
They say he’s already run his best
races, and he may have peaked.

“But all I know is that he’s done
everything we.ever asked of him.
He’s raced 10 times, and won seven
times, and he’s been out of the mon-
ey only once, and that was when he
stumbled coming out of the starting
gate. He’s a fine colt, and he knows
what he’s supposed to be doing out
there. He knows how to win.”

Fly So Free certainly knew how to
win last year, when he won the
Breeders’ Cup Juvenile by three
lengths and then the Eclipse Award
as the country’s best 2-year-old colt.

Meadow Star was voted the best 2-
year-old filly, but she won’t chall

him Saturday: She ran fourth in the
April 20 Wood Memorial in her first
encounter with colts and was with-
drawn as a candidate for the Derby.

Fly So Free kept on winning this
year, too. He even swept the Gulf-
stream Triple, winning the Hutche-
son Stakes, Fountain of Youth and
Florida Derby in a six-week burst of
brilliance.

But the brilliance seemed to lose a
little luster when he ran second to
Strike the Gold in the Blue Grass
Stakes two weeks ago. And now he
faces the ultimate test and the nag-
ging notion that suggests he might be
joining those 11 other horses since
Spectacular Bid who fought the long
odds against champions and did not
win.
