The Yolo Side — Only the dar-
ing need apply, and solo runs are not
advised. Start at the Crocker Art Mu-
seum and cross the Capitol Mall
bridge on the downstream side, then
slip around and under the bridge,
where you will encounter your first
homeless camp, and head upstream
along the trail.

This trail can be followed all the
way to what once was Bryte and now
is part of the new city of West Sacra-
mento, Stop when you get to the
fence of a state water project yard,
about 3.5 miles. From the top of the
levee at the fence you will have a
stunning view of Sacramento's
emerging skyline.

Many homeless people congregate
in this area. James O'Leary fought
his river battle here. On a recent
run, two homeless women emerged
from their camp in the bushes along
the river to ask my partner and | if
we had “seen a man with a gun.”
Dogs are numerous, as are junked
automobiles, trash and hobo camps.

This is truly a no-man’s land; one
I might add, that will disappear
when the newly incorporated city of
