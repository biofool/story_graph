Instead of boring winners, the Los Angeles Rams were
exciting losers Sunday. The switch cost them first place
in the NFC West but may be beneficial in the long run.

Rams Coach John Robinson brought in rookie Jim Ev-
erett and dusted off his passing offense Sunday. Everett
completed 12 of 19 for 193 yards and two touchdowns, but
it was a ‘‘Hail Mary” pass by New England’s Tony Eason
to Irving Fryar that stood out in a day of long passes

Fryar’s catch of the 25-yard deflected pass in the end
zone as time ran out gave the Patriots a 30-28 victory over
the Rams at Anaheim Stadium.

Eason established club records with 36 completions in
52 attempts and threw for 375 yards.

“Needless to say it was a difficult ending to a most ex-
citing day,” Robinson said. ‘‘I didn’t see the last play but
the game was obviously in doubt throughout.

“Tt was fun,’’ Eason said. ‘You're throwing the ball up
for grabs and you're hoping that something good will hap-
pen. Basically, the plan is if you can catch it, catch it; if
not tip it to someone else. ;

“T’ve never won a game like that before.”’

Eason scrambled around left end for 26 yards to the
Rams 25 to set up the final play. On the next play he
floated a pass that was deflected by cornerback Jerry
Gray to Fryar, who managed to get both feet in bounds.

“This play has never been successful in practice,
Fryar said. ‘‘We work on it two times a week. ,

Fryar’s 6-yard TD pass from Eason brought the Patri-
ots within 28-23 with 7:06 left.

Everett, who sat for seven games while Los Angeles
struggled on offense, replaced starter Steve Dils in the
second quarter. His TD passes went for 34 and 20 yards to
Henry Ellard, who caught eight passes for 129 yards. The
offense was a welcome sight for the Rams, who lost 6-0 to
New Orleans last week and entered the game last in the
NFL in passing offense. ;

“T know the guys are feeling down,” Everett said. “But
I feel good about the offensive side. Those things will hap-
pen, but we’ll bounce back.”

The Rams fell to 7-4 and trail San Francise by percent-
age points in the NFC West. The Patriots won their fifth
straight to improve to 8-3. ;

Eric Dickerson gained 102 yards on 24 carries for the
Rams. It was his seventh 100-yard game of the season,
and the first that Los Angeles lost.

In other games Sunday, Denver blasted Kansas City 38-
17, the Los Angeles Raiders clipped Cleveland 27-14, Chi-
cago shaded Atlanta 13-10, the New York Jets outlasted
Indianapolis 31-16, Dailas shaded San Diego 24-21, New
Orleans dumped St. Louis 16-7, the New York Giants
nicked Minnesota 22-20, Cincinnati slammed Seattle 34-7,
Miami cracked Buffalo 34-24, Detroit edged Philadelphia
13-11, Pittsburgh drilled Houston 21-10 and Green Bay
slammed Tampa Bay 31-7.

On Monday night, it’s San Francisco at Washington.
Broncos 38, Chiefs 17
At Denver, the Broncos forced two Bill Kenney fumbles
and turned them into 14 points within 17 seconds of the
first period to improve to 9-2 and move two games ahead
of the Chiefs and Raiders in the AFC West. Andre Towns-

end returned a Kenney fumble 8 yards for one Denver
score and Tony Colorito recovered another at the Chiefs
L
Raiders 27, Browns 14
At Los Angeles, Jim Plunkett, who will turn 39 in three
weeks, threw three touchdown passes to lift the Raiders.
Plunkett threw scoring tosses of 46 and 43 yards to Dokie
Williams and 3 yards to Todd Christensen.
Bears 13, Falcons 10
At Atlanta, Mike Tomezak rebounded from a miserable
first half to score a touchdown and pass the Bears into po-
sition for a field goal in the second half. Tomezak set up
his 1-yard touchdown run with an 85-yard pass to Emery
Moorehead. His 27-yard pass to Willie Gault set up Kevin
Butler’s game-winning, 44-yard field goal.
Jets 31, Colts 16
At East Rutherford, N.J., Ken O’Brien threw three
touchdown passes to Wesley Walker to help the Jets sur-
vive a scare from the NFL’s worst team. In notching their
ninth straight victory, extending their team record, the
Jets improved to an NFL-best 10-1. The Colts, 0-11, are
within three defeats of tying the club mark for most
losses in a row set in 1981.
Cowboys 24, Chargers 21
At San Diego, Steve Pelluer shook off 12 sacks and ran
for a 2-yard touchdown with 1:37 left to lift the Cowboys to
a comeback victory. Leslie O’Neal sacked Pelluer five
times and helped the Chargers tie an NFL record for
sacks.
Saints 13, Cardinals 7
At St. Louis, Morten Andersen kicked three field goals
to help New Orleans win its fifth game in six weeks and
improve to 6-5. Rueben Mayes ran 25 times for 131 yards
for the Saints. The Cardinals fell to 2-9.
Giants 22, Vikings 20
At Minneapolis, Raul Allegre kicked five field goals, in-
cluding a 33-yarder with 12 seconds left to lift New York.
With New York trailing 20-19, Allegre kicked the game-
winner to lift New York to 9-2, a half-game ahead of
Washington in the NFC East.
Bengals 34, Seahawks 7
At Cincinnati, Boomer Esiason passed for 334 yards
and set up three scores with bombs of 57, 54 and 36 yards
to lift the Bengals. Esiason completed 22 of 33 passes to
help Cincinnati improve to 7-4 and into a tie with Cleve-
land atop the AFC Central. The Seahawks lost their
fourth straight to fall to 5-6.
Dolphins 34, Bills 24
At Orchard Park, N.Y., Dan Marino threw four touch-
down passes to rally the Dolphins. Miami, 5-6, scored 24
second-half points and went ahead 27-24 3:05 into the
fourth quarter when Marino threw a 4-yard touchdown to
Dan Johnson.
Lions 13, Eagles 11
At Philadelphia, Eddie Murray kicked a 41-yard field
goal with 12 seconds left to lift the Lions.
Steelers 21, Oilers 10
At Pittsburgh, Mark Malone threw a 17-yard touch-
down pass to Rich Erenberg to cap a 21-point first half
and lead the Steelers.
Packers 31, Buccaneers 7
At Milwaukee, Randy Wright threw three touchdown
passes, including two to Phillip Epps, to lift the Packers.
