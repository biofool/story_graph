Askew Names Manasota Water Basin Board

The Manasota Water Board is in business, at last,
but Manatee County officials weren’t certain Monday what
the board’s first order of business will be, or when it
will occur.

Presumably, the nine members ~— five from Manatee,
four from Sarasota — will convene soon to elect officers
and become oriented on their responsibilities.

Gov. Reubin Askew’s office announced the appointments
in a letter county commissioners received Monday.

The governor’s action was all that will be necessary to
activate the board. And the board will give Manatee and
Sarasota what they wanted: control over ground and sub-
surface waters in the two counties.

Gov. Askew followed county commissioners’ recommenda-
tions on the appointments.

The Manatee board contingent includes: former Bradenton
Mayor A. Sterling Hall, Manatee Izaak Walton League
President Marty Farman, farmer-rancher Blake Whisenant,
business executive Ken Barneby and rancher J. Lynn Har-
rison.

Sarasota members are former Sarasota City Councilman
Kenneth Brumbaugh, columnist-conservationist James D
Neville, Mrs. Mary A. Kumpe and rancher-conseryationist
Latimer H. Turner.

Manatee commissioners originally speculated the board
would last until July, 1975, when Manatee and Sarasota
are scheduled to become members of the Southwest Florida
Water Management District (SFWMD),

However, Gov. Askew designated terms of up to three
years for some board members, indicating the board will

j \

remain intact — as a sub-district — even after SFWMD
takes in the two counties.

Hall, Mrs. Farman and Neville all received three-year
appointments; Whisenant, Mrs. Kumpe and Turner will serve
two years. Brumbaugh, Barneby and Harrison were alloted
one-year terms.

County Commission Chairman Ken Burton, to whom the
appointment letter was addressed, said he’s not definite
on the newly-created board’s next step.

“T assume they will meet, elect a chairman and other
officers. I don’t know who would call the meeting — us
or them,” Burton said.

County Pollution Control Director O.E. “Bud” Randle said
the basin board’s first meeting may be called by the Florida
Department of Natural Resources (DNR), which oversees
water district operations in all parts of the state.

“T assume the board will be notified by the DNR on
the time of an organizational meeting. They also may be filled
in on their duties, rules and regulations,’ Randle said.

Manatee County Atty. Richard Hampton said he also wasn't
clear on what procedures the board will follow immediately.

The letter confirming the board appointments made no
reference to a Manatee-Sarasota request for $25,000 in initial
administrative funds.

The counties sought the monies so the basin board could
function administratively for the remainder of the 1973-74
fiscal year.

State officials said they'd attempt to obtain the $25,000
