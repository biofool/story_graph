Sierra Club outings make it easy to explore —

Are you in the market for new places to explore
or new wilderness activities to try? Or are you
simply interested in meeting other people who
share your love of the outdoors?

In any case, Sierra Club outings may be just for

ou.

Each weekend, the Mother Lode Chapter of the
Sierra Club offers a variety of trips geared to a
variety of abilities. Most outings are daylong hikes
in state and national parks and in backcountry
areas from the California coast to the High Sierra.

These adventures involve car pooling to the
trailhead, are free (participants, though, chip in
for gas) and are open to anyone (if you're not a
Sierra Club member, however, you might want to
consider joining this environmental group).

In Sacramento, pick up the Mother Lode Chap-
ter’s quarterly outings schedule free at such out-
door shops as Alpine West, REI or Sierra
Outfitters. Then, choose a hike you think you can
handle, call the leader if you have any questions
(most trips don’t require advance signups, though
overnighters and special-skills outings usually
do), gather your gear (lunch, water, good walking
shoes or hiking boots, daypack, and assorted oth-
er essentials) and go for it

The treks take place rain or shine, and are rat-
ed according to mileage and elevation gain. For

example, a “1A” trip covers up to 6 miles and less
than 1,000 feet gain, while a vigorous “3C” outing
entails 10-15 miles and 2,000-3,000 feet gain. The
schedule describes ail the hiking classifications.

Sierra Club outings also include backpacking,
climbing, bicycle tours, river tours, car camping
and gorge scrambling (the latter involves rock
hopping, swimming and the like). As with day
hikes, these pursuits come in different degrees of
difficulty. Instructional trips and evening how-to-
get-started sessions are occasionally offered.

Special sections within the Mother Lode Chap-
ter include Inner City Outings (designed to pro-
vide youth and visually, physically and mentally
impaired people with “an opportunity to discover
that California is more than asphalt and con-
crete”) and Sierra Club Singles. You'll find infor-
mation on both groups in the chapter schedule.

Check the last page of the schedule for trip tips
and a rundown of rules — for instance, follow the
leader, and no dogs or radios allowed. Of course,
you'll also be expected to abide by the Sierra

Club’s guiding standard for wilderness etiquette:
“Take only pictures, leave only footprints.” {

For more information on the Sierra Club, write:
Sierra Club Mother Lode Chapter, P.O. Box 1339,
Sacramento 95812-1335.
