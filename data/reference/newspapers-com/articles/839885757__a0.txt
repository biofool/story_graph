Peninsula hendezvous with Maribeth

Autumn’s night and day have same measurements

Coming in on fall’s official
down beat, the autumnal equi-
nox, a convivial medley takes
its inspiration from variety.
The in-honor-of category in-
cludes the dinner welcoming
home a duo of travelers, Mrs.
Herman Zischek and Mrs.
Ralph K. Davies. They have
returned to Woodside after
cruises on the Adriatic and
down the Rhine River. Satur-
day evening the Paul Diberts
and the Forrest S. Crockers
will entertain at a dinner at
the Crockers’ Atherton home
with guests numbering mem-
bers of “The Spanish Class”
and their husbands. “The Span-
ish Class,” under the tutel-

age of Mrs. Harry Pierson of
Redwood City, not only goes
in for linguistics. The study
of Spanish is the peripatetic
type that has taken the class

from the Tahoe place of Mrs.
Davies to the Puerta Vallarta
holiday house of Mrs. Zischke.

In honor of their houseguests
Dr. and Mrs. Wendell Killins
on Wednesday the Charles W.
Straubels hosted a cocktail
buffet at their Atherton resi-
dence. Formerly of Green Bay,
Wis., the onetime home of
the Straubels, Dr. and Mrs.
Killins have come on west to
reside in Carmel.

As a prelude to the tradi-
tional September luau at the
Menlo Circus Club Thursday
night the Emmett Cashins Jr.
entertained at cocKtals at their
Atherton home.

With the duck season wait-
ing in the wings, it flies into
the scene Oct. 19 and continues
through Jan. 2, 1969, the Sam
Whitings Jr. of Portola Valley
have invited a group of nim-

rods and their wives for din-
ner on Oct. 11.

The unusually long season
means the weekend of Oct. 11
is the last before distaff mem-
bers of duck hunting house-
holds become “duck widows.”
Dove season and duck season
don’t quite dovetail for the
dove season ends the end of
this month. Incidentally it has
been a good season for Sam
Whiting. The Oct. 11 affair will
be a dove dinner.

Ann and John Huneke have
reserved Oct. 12 for cocktails
at their Menlo Park home.
Saturday evening the Richard
B. Hammonds have invited
friends for cocktails at the
Hammonds hillside home in
Ladera.

Up Ladera way too Nancy
(Mrs. Douglas) Tuck heads
the committee planning Fiesta
Ladera, the autumn rite of

the Ladera Oaks Swim and
Tennis Club. Instead of the
club’s customaryluau this year
the do takes its theme from
Mexico and the Olympics. It
will be staged a week from
Saturday at the club.

Back from an island inter-
lude at their Maui home are
the Paul Brownes of Wood-
side. Also back on the main-
land are the Brownes’ daugh-
ter and son-in-law, the Robert
J. Arthurs of Atherton, and
their daughter, Alison. The Ar-
thur trio were with the Brow-
nes at Maui before going on
to Mauna Kea.

Did you know that “sailing,
sailing” is the new hobby of
the George L. Longs Jr. of
Woodside? Longtime, and still,
golf buffs they have added
sailing to their sports enthu-
siasms with the acquisition of

a 30-foot boat they berth in
Sausalito.

A salute to courage from
one who knows just how much
it takes to accept confinement
to a “striker frame” has
come to the courageous Anne
(Mrs. Richard) Melbye. The
salute came in the form of a
letter to Anne from Sen, Ted
Kennedy, who after a plane
crash several years ago aiso
spent weeks in a “striker
frame.” As of Saturday it will
be eight weeks since Anne,
Dick and the William Owens
of Atherton were in a private
plane that crashed in the Rock-
ies. Anne spent five weeks
in the frame but now on week-
ends is permitted to leave the
Palo Alto-Stanford Hospital for
a visit of two or three hours

to the Melbyes’ Atherton home.

There is an air of mystery

about the encouraging missive
Anne received from Sen, Ken-
nedy for the Melbyes never
have met him nor do they
know of any mutual friends.
But his encouragement and
understanding have meant
much to Anne. It was his sug-
gestion that she take up hob-
bies compatible with being
confined to quarters. His own
hobby, he said, was painting.
So now Anne’s friends not only
continue to shower her with
books and flowers but have
added painting gear and nee-
dlecraft kits.

While Anne has her hospi-
tal session, which will contin-
ue for some weeks more,
Dick’s aunt, Miss Mary Wil-
liamson, has come on from
South St. Paul, Minn., to take
charge of the Melbyes’ house-
hold.
