By KAY BIRD
The New Mexican Staff

The director of the Teen Wellness Centers for
Santa Fe’s public high schools is spending her
summer wondering where to get the money for

_a counseling program that helps troubled
students.

But even if Director Alice Ephraim cannot
find the money to pay for the program, it will
continue, she said.

“We're not going to drop the counseling
program; no way,” Ephraim said.

A $26,000 grant provided for the past school
year by the state Human Services Department
wasn't renewed for next year. That money will
be gone June 30.

About 20 students still are being seen by
counselors through the end of the month.

After that, the students will be counseled at
such organizations as the Santa Fe Guidance
Center, Santa Fe Rape Crisis Center and Youth
Network.

Most of the other programs offered by the
centers ceased for the summer when the school
year ended on June 1.

The centers at Santa Fe High and Capital high
schools provide emotional and health counsel-
ing and information about contraceptives to
students. Medical examinations and prescrip-
tions also are offered at the clinics.

Many of the students coming in for counsel-
ing were depressed, abused, considering sui-
cide, having problems at home or dealing with
grief, Ephraim said.

Ephraim and representatives of Maternal and
Child Health Center, which sponsors the
centers, will spend the summer searching for
grants and asking local businesses and individu-
als for contributions.

If the fund-raising fails, the center will try to
get volunteer counselors to meet some of the
needs of the students, as it did when the
program first began in February 1989, she said.

The counselors were able to volunteer only
an hour or two a week, not enough to provide
consistent counseling to the students who
needed it, Ephraim said. “That's how we saw
we had to get some paid people in.”

Next year, when the state again is accepting
bids for funds, the centers will submit a

proposal. ;
