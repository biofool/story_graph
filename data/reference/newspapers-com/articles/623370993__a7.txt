Slopes the setting for two terrific tours

Adventurer Steve Barnett has long been in-
trigued with the idea of “skiing freely everywhere
in the snow-covered mountains.”

In his new book, “The Best Ski Touring in Amer-
ica” (Sierra Club, $10.95), Barnett describes 31
unique backcountry tours.

The routes cover all degrees of difficulty, from
day trips suitable for anyone to multi-day tra-
verses in deep wilderness. Areas include Olym-
pic, Arches, Grand Canyon and Yellowstone
national parks, Minnesota’s Boundary Waters,
and even Baja California and the Canadian
Rockies. Of special interest are these two Califor-
nia treks:

® Mount Shasta: This tour requires advanced
skiing skills, along with alpine touring equipment
or backcountry gear with good boots and down-
hill-oriented telemark skis. Ice ax and crampons
should also be carried.

“Like most of the cone-shaped Cascade volca-
noes, Mount Shasta is skiable on every side. There
are several routes down from its summit that of-
fer runs of 6,000 or more.

“Access is easy, and in the spring or early sum-
mer you may well find good corn snow for the en-
tire descent. The spring weather is considerably
better than it is on the large peaks farther north.”

Mount Shasta is an ideal mountain for ski
mountaineers who consider the downhill run par-
amount. The goal is to “work hard to stand on a
summit that is way above everything else in sight,
natural, or manmade, and then to enjoy an hour
or two of blissful concentration skiing down those
seemingly endless slopes you just spent six hours
climbing up.”

® Sierra Crest Traverse: This 250-mile-bong
route follows the Sierra Nevada crest from Car-
son Pass to south of Mount Whitney.

The tour covers the whole range of skiing —

from easy cross country to steep alpine. As with
most long ski traverses, however, “navigation,
camping and general mountaineering skills are
more important than particular expertise with
skis.” In fact, it’s always possible to take the skis
off for a bad section.

What makes the trek special? “The Sierra has a
perfect mix of beautiful alpine mountain scenery,
reliable snow, reliable sun, excellent ski terrain,
open forest, easy access, and an unbroken high
crest for hundreds of miles.”

For each tour in the book, Barnett provides all
the essential information for getting there and be-
ing there. He also includes general tips on safety,
clothing, equipment and skiing technique.

A major reason for “The Best Ski Touring
America,” says the author, is “to convey the ex-
citement, adventure and beauty that make ski
touring a great sport, especially to those readers
who are not yet hooked.”

The natural world — Joseph Cornell, au-
thor of the classic, “Sharing Nature with Chil-
dren,” has come up with another winner — this
time, a nature-awareness book geared to adults.

“Listening to Nature” (Dawn Publications;
$11.95), says the author, is designed to help you
absorb the beauty and serenity of natural places.

The large-format paperback book, laced with
beautiful photography, is arranged as a monthly
diary that can be used over and over again. Each
day’s pages offer an inspiring quotation, often
with an activity or explanation “to help you trans-

late the idea into personal experience.” :

“You do not have to be in the wilderness to do
these activities,” Cornell writes. “In fact, youcan |
do many of them while driving or walking to
work. As you use the activities more and more,
your receptivity will increase, and you'll begin to
see beauty in the most common things.”
