By Marjie Lambert
Bee Staff Writer

On the edge of Rocklin, beneath a grassy field
where hawks hunt for mice and rabbit, lies an
ancient riverbed lined with deposits of volcanic
rock.

R.C. Collet Inc. bought the land 10 years ago,
planning to mine the rock and crush it into grav-
el

Collet, a 25-year-old aggregate and construc-
tion company based in Woodland, let the land sit
idle while it mined other property. When it final-
ly applied for a county permit in 1985, it let the
application languish. By the time Collet serious-
ly began pursuing plans for a Rocklin mine, the
site was ringed by people who would become
opponents.

To the north are the new homes of Stanford
Ranch. To the south is the undeveloped fringe of
the city of Roseville and the proposed site of a
regional mall.

“It’s just too late,” said Wayne Dickey, who
moved to the new Stanford Ranch subdivision in
the summer of 1988. “They opted to wait, quite
long, until the residential development was in
place. If (Collet) had done this in 1982, Stanford
Ranch would not have taken off as fast.”

Even Collet officials admit that they made
their situation more difficult by waiting so long
— until 1988 — to seek permits to develop the
land as a mine.

“A few years ago, there was nothing out here

but me and some cattle,” said Mark Hirzy, Col-
let’s plants manager and a geologist. If the com-
pany had acted just two years earlier, the mine
could have been in operation before the first
homes were built in Stanford Ranch, and at least
some of the controversy could have been headed
off, he said.
